What is on the exam

  • Unit 1 and Unit 2 (microeconomics), as listed in the checklist.
  • Not on this exam: the circular flow of income, equity in the distribution of income and wealth, and market structures, including perfect competition.

1. Course guide checklist

These lines come from the IB course guide. Rate each one: R = I can't do this yet, A = I partly can, G = I can do this confidently. "Calculation" lines mean you must be able to calculate it from data.

Rated everything? Mark Part 1 as done, then move on.

2. Graph checklist

Draw each graph on paper from memory. Then check it against the textbook or Kognity and fix it in a different colour. Tick both boxes.

  • Label both axes and every curve.
  • Use P1, Q1 for the start and P2, Q2 for the new point.
  • A diagram is not finished until you can explain it in words.

3. Tricky differences

These pairs often get mixed up. Explain each difference in one or two sentences. Where it says so, sketch a quick diagram on paper. Rate how confident you are, then check against the textbook or Kognity.

4. 10-mark questions

Paper 1 part (a) style. Choose one and write your answer on paper, in about 20 minutes.

Structure

  1. Definitions: define the key terms in the question.
  2. Diagram: draw an accurate, fully labelled diagram.
  3. Explanation: explain the theory step by step, referring to your diagram.
  4. (Example): only if it helps. A general example is fine. It does not need to be real-world.

More help on answer structures

Questions

  1. Using a diagram, explain the likely consequences of a price ceiling (maximum price) in the market for rented housing.
  2. Using a diagram, explain why the free market may overallocate resources to the consumption of a demerit good such as cigarettes.
  3. Using a diagram, explain why a firm selling a good with price inelastic demand may increase its total revenue by raising its price.
