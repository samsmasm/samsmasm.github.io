# Sums, `/sums`

Maths practice for Sam's two daughters, written by finger or stylus on a touch screen. Built 2026-11-15. Handwriting is read **on the device** by a small neural network: no API, no network calls, works offline once loaded. Not linked from any index; `noindex`. A plain-English project note for Sam is in the Obsidian vault at `Projects/Sums/Sums.md` (update it when the plan changes).

**Users.** A 5-year-old in US-style kindergarten who **can't read yet** (pictures, numbers and spoken prompts only, never written questions she must read) and a nearly-8-year-old in grade 2 working above level (she found the first age-8 set straightforward). Sam wants them free to explore: the girls change their own settings, nothing is locked.

**Organised by NZ curriculum year.** `CURRICULUM.md` lists the NZ Years 0–6 maths curriculum (October 2025 teaching sequences, re-checked against the PDFs 2026-11-16) with each Sums activity ticked against it, and "Out of step" notes where a game does something in a different year (× ÷ signs in Year 1, "quarter to" in Year 2, long division before Year 5). `NZC Maths Activity Alignment.md` is Sam's outside audit with ideas for new activities (Sam's file, untracked). Sam wants skills both **across** (everything for her year) and **down** (one skill through the years), and never to drop an activity because she has moved up. New activities are built "vertically": one activity with a **Year** option for each level (Years 1–4 for every year activity so far).

**Before committing:** run `test/run.sh` (all must pass; questions are random, so run it twice after layout changes). Update `tracker/projects.md` and run `python3 tracker/publish.py` (see the repo CLAUDE.md).

**Sam's look rules.** The sheet stays plain, like a paper printout (he likes it). Pictures are very clean and simple; block colours only where they add value (graph categories as colours a non-reader can match, a flat colour on a shape), never just for prettiness. The front screens can be more designed than the sheet. Not AESTHETIC.md.

## Flow
1. **Names** (opening screen): big name tiles, Everyone, "+ Add a name". Switching names clears the current sheet or grid, so nobody lands on someone else's.
2. **Age**: "How old are you?" with cake tiles, ages **4 to 11**, read aloud. Age is only a suggested starting point (Sam). A named player is asked once and the age is saved on the player (names from before ages existed are asked on first tap); **Everyone** is asked every time, so ages never mix. Store the age, not a grade. Age decides three things:
   - the tile set (`group()`): 4–6 the younger set (activities whose `ages` include 5), 7–11 the older set (`ages` include 8);
   - read-aloud (`reads()`): ages 4–6;
   - the **suggested year** (`yearFor`: age − 4, at least 1; so 4 and 5 → Year 1, 8 → Year 4, 11 → Year 7). Every activity with a Year option starts at `suggestedYear`: the highest year it has at or below hers, or its lowest year if it starts above hers (a new activity may have only Year 4 so far). The setup page marks it with a green dot and "Suggested for age 10: Year 6 (Year 4 is the highest here so far)" or "Suggested for age 7: Year 3 (Year 4 is the lowest here so far)". Her own saved choices still win.
3. **Home**: "What do you want to do today?" activity tiles for her tile set (picture, name, best time for her current settings). Read aloud at 4–6. Header: ‹ Names, speaker, "Name · age", Records.
4. Tapping an activity **with choices opens its setup page first** (Sam: ask every time), with that player's own last choices selected; title "Name · Activity"; at 4–6 it says "Choose, then press Start." Activities with no choices (Make 10, Teen numbers) go straight to the sheet. Back on a setup returns where she came from (home, or her sheet/grid via Start again).
5. **Sheet** (cards) or **grid** (Times grid): header ‹ Home, Start again (that activity's setup), speaker (repeats the question), score, **Working**, Mark. At 4–6 the question is spoken when the sheet opens.
   - **Working area** (Sam): "Working" opens a plain page to work things out: the bottom third in portrait, the right third in landscape (the sheet shrinks to the rest). Undo, Clear, Hide. Nothing on it is read or marked; it empties with each new sheet. Open or closed is remembered on the device (`sums-working`). Strokes are in pixels, so they don't move with rotation.
   - **Finished** (Sam: there was no obvious next step after a sheet was all right): 0.7 s after the last card turns green (so she sees the ticks), a panel over the sheet (header still usable): a green tick that pops in with a small burst of block colours (none with reduced motion), "Perfect!" (all first try) or "All done!" (said aloud at 4–6), the time, first try N of M, "New best!" or the best. Buttons: **Another sheet** ("Another grid" on the grid), **Harder: Year N** (only where there is a higher year; saves it as her choice), **Choose something else** (home), and a small "Look at my sheet" that closes it. `queueDone`, `showDone`, `hideDone`.
6. **Records** page: change age (cakes), best times and recent sheets for every activity, each with **Delete** (Sam: for when someone else did a sheet on her name). Deleting one recent sheet works its best time and count out again from what's left; deleting a best-time row removes every sheet of that kind. **Remove** (the person: records and settings too). All deletes are double-click proof (`arm`): the first tap turns the button into Keep, the real confirm appears at the top, ignoring taps for 0.5 s.

**Scrolling**: the front screens (names, age, home, setup, records) scroll the page itself with a sticky header; sheets and the grid lock the page (`body.lock`) and the sheet scrolls inside. Sam's Firefox would not scroll the home tiles inside the old fixed panels.

## Activities (`ACTS` in index.html)
Each has `name`, `ages` (tile set: 5 younger, 8 older), `setup` kind, `say` (spoken prompt), `pic` (tile SVG), `defaults(group)`, `make(cfg)` → question (`kind` + `ans`), `key(cfg)` / `label(key)` for records; optional `options`, `blocked(cfg)` (warning text that turns Start off), `grid`, `noCount`. A question can carry its own spoken words in `q.say` (`cardSay` uses it first).

**Year options.** Written `{ key: 'yr', values: [[1, "1 · o'clock"], ...] }`; the setup page shows "Year 1" in bold on its own line and the text after " · " as the example under it (`.yrb`; Sam: "1 · o'clock" made the 1 look like part of the example).

### Younger tiles (ages 4–6) only
- **How many?**: ten frame(s), dots filled in order (top row left to right). Up to 5 / 10 / 20 (20 = two frames, first full). No line under ten frames (Sam removed it); she writes anywhere below.
- **Number bonds**: whole circle on top joined to two part circles; one is empty (`.hole`) and she writes **inside it** (stroke centre within 1.9 em of the circle centre; radius 1 em). Up to 5 / 10 / 20. A part of 0 only about 1 card in 10.
- **Teen numbers**: 11–19 as a full ten frame plus a second frame with 1–9 dots.
- **Make 10**: one ten frame, 1–9 dots, empty spaces as dashed circles; answer = 10 − dots.
- **Read aloud**: at 4–6 every card has a speaker in its foot (`cardSay`: "3 plus 4", "3 and 4 make what?", "7 is 3 and what?"; frames use the activity's prompt).

### Older tiles (ages 7–11) only
- **Missing number**: `7 + ☐ = 12` with "☐ = ___" underneath. Hides the first or second number; never an equation where any number works (0 × ☐ = 0, 0 ÷ ☐). The line length comes from the longest number shown, so it gives no hint. Keypad setup.
- **Arrays**: r rows × c columns of dots, write the total. `grid` setup: the two keypads relabelled Rows / Columns, signs and "bigger" hidden; only 0 chosen → warning, Start off. Default rows 2–5, columns 2–9.
- **Place value**: kinds Blocks (base-ten, true proportions: hundreds squares 3 a row, tens sticks, ones cubes 3 a row; `PV_EM` sets the size), 300 + 40 + 7 (zero parts left out, shuffled about a third of the time so she can't just copy digits), 10 more / 10 less (amounts 1, 10, 100, and 1,000 at 9,999; never below 0 or above the max). Up to 99, 999 or 9,999. At 9,999 the thousands are cubes (3 a row, front face a hundred square) with the hundreds beside them and the tens and ones on a row underneath, so the picture stays about square (`pvLayout(h, t, o, k)`; `q.k` is the thousands).
- **Number lines**: 10 steps, only the two ends labelled, an arrow at tick 1–9; she works out the step. Counting in 1s / 2s / 5s / 10s / 25s / 50s / 100s / 1000s; the line never goes past 10,000. The SVG has room for a five-digit label at each end.
- **True or false** (`tf`, `tfQ`, tap card, Year 4 only so far): a number sentence, tiles ✓ and ✗ (spoken "true" and "not true"), about half true. Year 4: four-digit + and −, tables to 12 × 10, × 10 and ÷ 10, and both-sides sentences (6 × 4 = 12 × 2, 36 + 32 = 34 + 33). False ones are near misses: out by 1, 10 or 100, two digits swapped, a times-table neighbour, or a place out. Read aloud "8205 take away 4721 equals 3484. True?".
- **More or less** (`cmp`, `cmpQ`, tap card, Year 4 only so far): two sides with an empty box between them, tiles < = >. Kind Numbers (to 10,000, often the same digits in a different order, or one place apart), Sums (two close times-table facts, or thousands and hundreds: 3000 + 400 ☐ 3500 − 100); about one in five equal. Kind **Fractions** (`cmpFracQ`, printed top over bottom with `.fr`): same bottom number (3/8 ☐ 5/8, to twelfths), same top number (1/3 ☐ 1/5, mostly unit fractions), or tenths against a decimal (3/10 ☐ 0.5, 1/2 ☐ 0.5); only the decimal ones can be equal (about one in eight overall). Read aloud "3 over 8, and 5 over 8".
- **Fractions of a number** (`fof`, `fofQ`, `fofSvg`, frame card, Year 4 only so far): the bar model. Caption "1/4 of 28" or "1/4 of the set is 7" (stacked fraction), a brace over a bar of d equal boxes. Kind **Find the part** (the whole on the brace, "?" in the first box) or **Find the whole** (the number in the first box, "?" on the brace). Unit fractions 1/2 to 1/10; the part is 2–10 (tables) or, about 3 in 10, a round number (1/3 of 300), whole at most 1,000; boxes from 6 parts up hold two digits at most. Answer anywhere under the bar. Read aloud "What is a quarter of 28?", "An eighth of a set is 7. How many in the set?".
- **Balance** (`bal`, `balQ`, Year 4 only so far): an equation with one box, then "☐ = ___" (laid out like Number patterns). Year 4: four-digit + and − with a plain number on one side (4200 − ☐ = 4001), × 10 / ÷ 10 and table facts (☐ ÷ 10 = 12, 7 × ☐ = 56), and both sides to work out (36 + 17 = ☐ + 20, 6 × 8 = ☐ × 4, 64 − 25 = ☐ − 30, 250 + ☐ = 300 + 75); the box can be any of the four numbers; never the same number on both sides; sides swap half the time. Whole numbers 1 to 9,999, exactly one answer. Read aloud "36 plus 17 is the same as what plus 20?".
- **Decimals** (`decQ`, `dec` card, Year 4 only so far: tenths): Kinds **Picture** (`decBarsSvg`: full bars of ten are ones, then one bar with some tenths shaded, 0.1 to 2.9, always in the room of three bars; about one in four is instead the fraction link "3/10 =" or "1/2 =" as a stacked fraction), **Number line** (`decLineSvg`: 0 to 2 in tenths, only 0, 1, 2 labelled, halves a bit longer, an arrow, never on a whole number), **Add and take away** (1.3 + 0.2, 2.5 − 0.4, a third cross a whole; never a whole answer), **× and ÷ by 10** (23 ÷ 10 → two blanks; 2.3 × 10 → a plain `expr` card with one line). Values are kept in tenths (`q.v`, 13 = 1.3) so nothing is a float.
- **Rounding** (`roundQ`, Year 4 only so far): nearest 10, 100, 1,000 (numbers to 9,999) or tenths to the nearest whole number. Kind **Number line** (`rline`, `rlineSvg`: a 10-step line between the two candidates, both ends labelled, the number on an arrow at its place; "to the nearest 100" above; answer below; never exactly halfway) or **Just numbers** (`rnum`: the number big, "to the nearest 100" under it, then a line; halfway allowed, rounds up). A number already round is never asked. Answers can be 0 (0.3) or 10000 (9960).
- **Area**: kinds Count squares (grid, sides ≤ 10), Area (side lengths only), Perimeter, Missing side (caption "Area 36", "?" on a side). Sides up to 5 / 10 / 12. A caption above the rectangle says what to find; rectangle to proportion (`areaSvg`). Year 4 kinds (A6a, on by default; "Sides up to" doesn't touch them): **Odd shapes** (`oddQ`, `oddSvg`: 3–9 whole squares grown from one cell inside a 7 × 5 grid, plus 2, 4 or 6 half squares cut corner to corner, each in its own empty cell sharing a whole side with a square; one block colour, the full grid drawn faintly over it; "How many squares?") and **Cubes** (`cubesQ`, `cubesSvg`: a cuboid 2–4 across, 2–3 deep, 1–3 high, at least 6 cubes, drawn at an angle with every cube edge on the front, top and side; front in a block colour, top lighter, side darker via `tint`; "How many cubes?"). The card width allows for the caption (a flat cuboid made "How many cubes?" overflow).
- **Times bonds**: number bonds for multiplication (product on top, factors below, one empty; a missing factor is a division). Tables 2–12, up to × 10 or × 12. Reuses the `bond` card.
- **Fractions** (`fracQ`): Kinds **Shaded shapes** (a bar or circle cut into d equal parts with n shaded; bars sometimes shade scattered parts), **Number line** (0 to 1 cut into d, an arrow), **Past 1** (`q.wholes = 2`, caption "Write it as one fraction": two bars stacked, two circles side by side, or a 0-to-2 line with 0, 1, 2 labelled, a third of the time; n between d + 1 and 2d − 1, the first whole always full) and **Add and take away** (`form: 'add'`, `q.terms`, `q.op`: two or three fractions with the same bottom number, each with a small bar over it, "=" then the answer bar; about a third take away (proper minus proper); totals may pass one whole; never a whole-number total). Parts 2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12 (default 2,3,4,6,8); default all four kinds. Top number above a short printed bar, bottom number below (`fracParts`). Forgiving (Sam found real writing missed): a big top number may reach up into the picture (only marks entirely on the picture are ignored, unless they line up with a digit reaching down out of it); if everything lands on one side of the printed bar, it splits by her own fraction line, then two stacked rows with a gap, then a straight slash ("3/4" across); it never ignores all her writing. **Equal fractions count** (2/4 for 1/2). Reading shows "3/4", or "3/?" if one is missing.
- **Times grid** (Sam's design): its own screen (`#gridS`, `startGrid`), not cards. 1..N across and down, only the triangle row ≤ column (6 × 7 = 7 × 6 is one cell; diagonal included). A number appears on a tile; she **drags** it to its cell (or taps the cell). Any empty cell with that product counts (12 fits 2 × 6, 3 × 4, 1 × 12). Wrong cell: red flash, tile goes back, no longer first try. While dragging, the row and column headers light up. Cells outside her tables are grey and never asked. Choices: tables 1–12 (default 2–12), 10 × 10 or 12 × 12, numbers 12 / 24 / whole grid (`noCount`: no Questions row, no examples). Mark is hidden while playing and becomes "New grid" at the end. Cell size fills the screen (`sizeGrid`, on resize).

## The sheet (plain, like a paper printout; Sam likes it, keep it that way)
- Grid of cards follows rotation: portrait 2/3/4 columns for 6/12/20, landscape 3/4/5, phones in portrait always 2 (scrolls).
- `qMarkup(q)` prints each question; `--h` / `--c` (height and width in em) set the font size to fit the card. Layouts: `col` (stacked + − ×: carry space on top, sign left, line under), `div` (long-division bracket, answer on the bar), `miss` (a line of text, then "= ___"; also `expr`, `seq`, `bal` and `rnum` cards), `story` (wrapped words, then "___ unit"), `bond`, `frac`, `pic2` (clocks, money, graphs, shapes, rulers: question or picture, then blanks; `qMarkup` can name its own `anchor`; a `<br>` in a graph caption gives it two lines), `tap` (see below), and `frame` (any picture on top, answer anywhere below: ten frames, arrays, blocks, number lines, rounding lines, area, Year 1 doubles).
- Each card is one writing surface (`.work`, canvas over the printed question). Strokes are stored relative to an **anchor** in units of the printed font size, so writing stays lined up after rotation: `.rule` (col), `.dd` top (div), `.wl` (miss/expr/seq/story), centre of `.hole` (bond), `.fbar` (frac), `.ln` (single blank on pic2), the middle of ":" or "and" on the lines (two blanks), bottom of the picture (frame). The anchor and font size are **measured again at every pen-down** (`remeasure`; earlier strokes stay where she wrote them).
- **Which writing is the answer** (`answerStrokes` + `inZone`): below the line (col, frame), above the bar (div), inside the empty circle (bond), everything (miss/expr/seq/bal/rnum/story/pic2, so an answer on the line, in the box or a bit low all count), above/below the bar (frac). Ruled lines she draws are ignored; several rows → the bottom row. If a card has writing but none in its answer place, all her writing is read instead of skipping the card.
- **Two blanks** (`q.two`, `q.parts`): Clocks Year 2 up, Money Year 3 up, and Decimals (a printed point, `q.dp` = decimal places). Shown as "3:05", "$2 and 50c" or "1.3". Decimals: after the point digits keep their places (1.03 is not 1.3) but trailing zeros are forgiven (1.30); an empty first blank counts as 0 (".7"); if both numbers land on one side, the last `dp` digits are the decimal part. `twoParts` is forgiving (Sam found writing missed): strokes are grouped into digits (`digitClusters`) and each digit goes left or right of the middle; dots (her own colon or point, under 0.3 em) are not digits; writing more than 2.4 em up (on the clock or coins) is ignored unless it lines up with a digit below; if both numbers land on one side it splits by her own colon or point, else the last two digits are the minutes / cents; it never ignores all her writing. Money drops a "$" (taller than her digits) before and a "c" (smaller) after (`dropMoneySigns`), on single blanks too.
- `readAnswer(c)` reads and checks every card (`{ text, ok, backwards }`); fractions compare n·D = N·d. A reading error shows on the card as "? (Error: ...)" instead of nothing (`safeRead`; Mark skips that card).
- After a 0.5 s pause the reading shows in big grey text (26 px, Sam) under the card.
- **Mark marks every card with anything written on it, in one press** (Sam): 4 of 12 done → those 4 marked, clock keeps going; the sheet finishes when all are right.
- **Wrong** (Sam): only "Try again" (clears the answer, keeps working); the answer is never shown. When a later try is right, the card shows "first read N" and **I was right the first time**, which counts it as first try right (and updates the saved history if the sheet is already finished).
- **Backwards digits**: the reader has 17 classes, 0–9 plus BACKWARDS 2 3 4 5 6 7 9, because a flip check can't tell a backwards 5 from a real 2 (tested: same confidence). A backwards digit still counts; the card shows it mirrored next to the right way round, and at 4–6 it is said ("Watch out, your 3 is backwards"). Sam briefly floated a "receptive" version (choose which way round is right) and dropped it.
- Palm rejection once a stylus is used (cards and the working area). Undo removes the last stroke.
- **Tap cards** (`kind: 'tap'`, built in A2): `{ top (html), tc / th (its width and height in em), tiles: [{ html, label, say }], right (index, or indices with multi), say }`, optional `multi` and `cells` (tiles as a grid, `cells` across). The card gets class `tapc`: no canvas, Undo or Clear, the `.qv` takes clicks. Tile size comes from the card, not the font (`--tw` from `cqh`/`cqw`, 40–84 px, cells 30–60 px), so every card on a sheet has the same big tiles; the words keep to the top 45% (30% over a grid). A tap picks a tile (`c.pick`, thick inset border, `paintTap`), another tap moves it (multi: on and off); it starts the clock. At 4–6 a tapped tile says its `say`, and the card speaker reads `q.say` then every tile. `readAnswer` gives the chosen labels and `ok` (multi: all and only the right ones). Mark leaves the reading blank (the tile shows it); wrong is the red border and Try again, which clears the choice; no "I was right the first time". The Working area works as usual. `writeNum` in the tests just picks the right tiles.
- Look: black and white; the only colour is dark green / dark red card borders, grey fraction shading, and block colours in the Graphs, Shapes, Rulers and Money pictures.

## Setup page (Start again)
- `keypad` (Sums, Missing number): laid out like a sum. ### Two new card kinds (built once, in the step that first needs them)
**`tap` card** (step A2). Question or picture on top, 2 to 4 tiles in a row underneath (bordered boxes holding a sign, a short word, a number or a small SVG). Tapping a tile selects it (thick ink border); tapping another moves the choice; nothing is marked until Mark, like written cards. `readAnswer` returns the chosen tile's label and `ok`. Wrong: red border and Try again, which clears the choice. No "I was right the first time" (nothing is being read). No writing canvas on the card, but the Working area still works. At 4–6 the card speaker reads the question, then each tile in order. With only two tiles a second try gives it away; that is fine because only the first try scores. Options: `q.multi` (several tiles can be on; all and only the right ones must be chosen) and `q.cells` (tiles laid out as a grid of small squares instead of a row; used by Map, Mirror and the coordinate plane).

**Two blanks with other signs** (step A4). `q.two` already handles ":" and "and". Add a printed "." (decimals, "$ __ . __"), "r" (remainders, "__ r __") and "h / min" ("__ h __ min"). A decimal's second blank must keep leading zeros: 1.05 is right only as "05", unlike minutes where 5 = 05 is forgiven for the first blank only. Trailing zeros are forgiven (0.5 = 0.50).

### Phase F: Year 6

**F1. Year 6 number.**

**F2. Year 6 measuring, shapes, data, chance.**
- *Plot* (new, tap card with `q.cells` on line crossings): x and y axes 0 to 10. "Tap (3, 2)", including points on an axis; then a rule in words ("start at 2, add 3 each time") with a table missing two values (written), then tap the points.

## Files
- `index.html`: everything, inline CSS/JS. Globals used by tests include `ACTS`, `makeQ`, `cards`, `buildSheet`, `layout`, `sizeCanvas`, `draw`, `read`, `readAnswer`, `answerStrokes`, `fracParts`, `twoParts`, `paintTap`, `loadCfg`, `loadRec`, `labelFor`, `draft`, `players`, `current`, `act`, `age`, `sheetStart`, `goNames`, `G`, `startGrid`, `dropOn`, `W` (working area), and each activity's generator (`graphQ`, `shapeQ`, `rulerQ`, `seqQ`, `dblQ`, `storyQ`, `addSubQ`, `mulDivQ`, `tfQ`, `cmpQ`, `balQ`, `roundQ`, `decQ`, `decTwo`, `fracQ`, `cmpFracQ`, `fofQ`), `countsOf`, `cfgStoreKey`.
- `CURRICULUM.md`: NZ Years 0–6 maths curriculum with what Sums covers.
- `mock/`: the big-grid mock (see above).
- `digits.js`: the reader. `readNumber(strokes, unit)` splits strokes into digits (x-overlap grouping, or a thin stroke inside a wider one such as a 4's down stroke), renders each MNIST-style (20 px box, centre of mass at 14,14, stroke radius 1.25 px), runs the CNN in plain JS, and returns `{ text, digits, backwards }`.
- `model.js`: int8 weights (~80 KB). CNN conv16/conv32/conv64 (3×3, relu, maxpool) → fc64 → fc17. `digits.js` reads the class count from the weights, so a 10-class model still works.
- `train/train.py`: retrains on MNIST with augmentation, mirroring a quarter of each batch into the backwards classes. Needs torch (`pip install --target <dir> torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`, run with `PYTHONPATH=<dir>`) and the MNIST .gz files in `train/mnist/` (gitignored). `python3 train.py <out.js>` (~10 min on CPU). Last run (2026-11-15): 99.2% digits, 98.3% backwards digits. `train/tojson.py ../test/mnist_sample.json` makes the parity sample (gitignored).
- `test/run.sh`: node reader tests (synthetic pen strokes, backwards digits, splitting, JS = PyTorch parity), then headless-Chrome tests of every screen and activity (speech stubbed and checked). `SUMS_SHOTS=<dir>` saves screenshots.
- **Firefox** (Sam's browser) can be driven by the same puppeteer-core: `puppeteer.launch({ browser: 'firefox', executablePath: '/usr/lib/firefox/firefox', headless: true })` (not `/usr/bin/firefox`, a shell wrapper). Touch works with `hasTouch` + `page.touchscreen` in both browsers.

## Known limits and open issues
- **Open (2026-11-15):** on Sam's own device Fractions, Clocks and Money detected no writing at all, even tidy writing (patterns, doubles, story sums were fine). Not reproduced here in Chrome or Firefox, with mouse or touch. Safety nets added (re-measure at pen-down, never ignore all writing, errors shown on the card). Waiting on: does ink appear, what shows in grey under the card, which device, finger or stylus.
- Synthetic tests are neater than a child's writing; real accuracy is unknown until they use it on the tablet. If a digit is misread a lot, a "save sample" mode could collect their strokes to fine-tune.
- Digits written touching or overlapping a lot can merge into one.
- Speech uses the device's built-in voice (speechSynthesis); quality varies by tablet.
- The page doesn't update itself while open: reload after each change (Sam saw old behaviour from an unrefreshed tab).
