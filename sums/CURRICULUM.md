# NZ maths curriculum, Years 0 to 6 (for planning Sums)

Source: the Ministry of Education's October 2025 year-by-year teaching sequences, *Mathematics and Statistics* Years 0–1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Re-checked line by line against those six PDFs on 2026-11-16 (with `NZC Maths Activity Alignment.md`). Where an earlier version of this file differed, the PDFs won: named quadrilaterals moved from Year 4 to Year 6, parallel lines from Year 4 to Year 5, and Years 5 and 6 were added. Probability starts in Year 5.

**Years.** Year 1 = first year at school (turns 5 to 6); its first six months are "Year 0" here. Year 2 = 6–7, Year 3 = 7–8, Year 4 = 8–9, Year 5 = 9–10, Year 6 = 10–11. So the 5-year-old (US kindergarten) is Year 0 going on Year 1, and the nearly-8-year-old (US grade 2, above level) is Year 3 going on Year 4, with Years 5 and 6 as her way up.

**Marks.** ✓ = a Sums activity already covers it (name in brackets). ✋ = hands-on (real objects, moving around, class surveys), so it needs adapting to fit a screen. Everything else could be a new activity. "Out of step" = the game does it in a different year from the curriculum.

Each year lists **skills** (what she does). Facts she needs to know come after "knows:".

---

## Year 1 (first year at school)

Year 1 starts with a "first six months" stage (Year 0); those skills are marked *(6m)*.

**Year 0 is only this:** numbers to 20, counting to 10 objects, subitising 3–5, more / less / same, facts to 5, the number before and after to 10, ordinals to 5th, a fully labelled number line, two-element repeating patterns, comparing two objects directly, days of the week, sorting triangles, circles, rectangles and squares, and following directions. **Not yet in Year 0:** numbers past 20, counting in 2s and 10s, facts past 5, doubles, equal groups, fractions, money, clocks, number sentences, three-element patterns, 3D shapes and graphs.

### Number
- Read and write numbers to 20 *(6m)*, then to 100. ✓ (Teen numbers, Place value)
- Count forwards and backwards from any number, within 10 then 20 *(6m)*, then within 100. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 1)
- Count forwards and backwards in 2s and 10s, 0–100. Knows: counting in 2s from 0 gives even numbers, from an odd number gives odd. ✓ (Number patterns, Number lines)
- Say the number before or after, to 10 *(6m)* then 20.
- Compare and order numbers to 20 *(6m)*, then to 100; ordinals 1st to 5th *(6m)*, then with suffixes (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) to 100. ✓ partly (More or less, Year 1: two numbers to 20; not ordering, ordinals or to 100)
- Put numbers on a fully labelled number line *(6m)*, then on a partly labelled one (17 on a line marked in 5s). ✓ partly (Number lines)
- Subitise (see without counting) 3–5 objects *(6m)*, then smaller groups inside a bigger set (3 and 5 in 8). ✓ (How many?, Kind Groups: two dice in two colours)
- Count up to 10 objects one by one *(6m)*, then up to 20, also in pairs. ✓ (How many?)
- More, less or the same *(6m)*. ✓ (More or less, Year 1: two heaps of dots, "same" tile)
- Find totals to 20 by grouping in pairs, 5s or 10s. Knows: ten ones is one 10; 12 is six pairs or one 10 and two ones. ✓ (Teen numbers)
- Numbers in te reo Māori to 30.

### Operations
- Addition and subtraction facts to 5 *(6m)*, then to 10 (including 10 + 0). ✓ (Sums, Number bonds, Make 10)
- Doubles and halves to 10. ✓ (Doubles and halves, Year 1)
- Add and subtract one- and two-digit numbers within 20, including 0. ✓ (Sums, Add and take away Year 1)
- Join and separate groups to 20 (9 + 6, 7 + __ = 11). ✓ (Missing number, Number bonds; Splat: "8 altogether", some dots under a blot, how many are hiding, totals 6 to 20)
- Add ten to a one-digit number (10 + 4). ✓ (Add and take away, Year 1)
- One-step + and − word problems with objects and pictures. ✓ (Story sums, Year 1, read aloud; no pictures)
- Multiply and divide by equal groups or sharing (counting objects), answers within 20. ✓ (Story sums, Arrays, Times and divide Year 1: bags of dots and sharing into empty bags, no signs; B1)
- Knows: adding 0 changes nothing; order doesn't matter when adding but does when subtracting. Only the + and − signs are Year 1.

### Fractions and money
- Halves and quarters of sets, amounts and shapes, using equal parts. ✓ (Halves and quarters, Year 1: tap the shape cut into equal parts with one shaded, against unequal cuts and other numbers of parts; Fractions)
- Find a half or quarter of a set by sharing; ½ of ½ is ¼. ✓ partly (Halves and quarters, Year 1, written: half of 8, a quarter of 12, as dots in groups with a dashed sharing line; ½ of ½ not asked)
- Know NZ coins and notes and their values. ✓ (Money, Year 1; Which coin?: "Tap the 50 cent coin" among coins and notes at their real relative sizes)

### Algebra (patterns and number sentences)
- Open number sentences with one-digit numbers (2 + 5 = 3 + __). ✓ (Balance, Year 1, over a see-saw; Missing number)
- True or false? (7 − 5 = 6 − 4) ✓ (True or false, Year 1, dots under the sides on about half)
- Copy, continue and make repeating patterns: two elements *(6m)* (cat, dog, cat, dog...), then three. ✓ partly (Picture patterns, Year 1: three-part repeats, find the gap; not making, two-part is Year 0)
- Find the missing element (red, green, blue, red, __, blue). ✓ (Picture patterns, Year 1: shape only, colour only, or both)
- Ordinals to 5th for positions in a pattern.

### Measurement
- Compare two things by length, weight or capacity, directly *(6m)*, then using a third object. Words: longer, shorter, taller, heavier, lighter. ✓ partly (Rulers, Year 1, Which is longer?: pencils that start in different places on a block grid, and towers of blocks; length and height only, not weight or capacity; no third object)
- Days of the week in order, the day before and after *(6m)*; weekdays and weekend.
- Tell the time to the hour (o'clock) on analogue and digital clocks. ✓ (Clocks, Year 1: write the hour, and Match: tap the digital clock for a face)
- Choose a sensible unit for how long things take (seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years).
- Put events of the day in order (before, after, yesterday, tomorrow); parts of the day (morning, noon, evening, midnight).

### Geometry
- Name and sort 2D shapes by one attribute: triangle, circle, rectangle, square *(6m)*. Knows: a shape keeps its name whatever its size or colour. ✓ (Shapes, Year 1: count sides and corners; Name: "Tap the triangle" among shapes of different sizes, colours and turns)
- Name and sort 3D shapes in different positions: cube, cylinder, sphere. ✓ (Shapes, Year 1, Name: cube, cylinder, sphere, with a flat look-alike among them)
- Make a shape from smaller shapes, and break one up.
- Position words: above, below, left, right, in front, behind, inside, outside, next to.
- Follow directions to a place or object ✋; describe positions with pictures or simple maps.

### Statistics
- Collect category data for a question (whose foot is longer?) ✋, and tally it.
- Make a picture graph (one picture per item, all the same height, stacked in columns).
- Read a picture graph: how many in each group, which has most or least. ✓ (Graphs, Year 1: columns of same-size circles on a line, how many, and tap the colour with the most or least; B1)

## Year 2

### Number
- Read, write and show numbers to 120 with tens and ones (and hundreds to 120). ✓ (Place value)
- Compare and order numbers to 120.
- Place value of each digit in two-digit numbers and three-digit numbers to 120. ✓ (Place value)
- Roughly place numbers on a number line marked in tens (61 on a line marked 0, 10, 20...). ✓ partly (Number lines)
- Count a collection to 120 by making groups of ten.
- Round numbers to 120 to the nearest 10.
- Count in 2s, 5s and 10s forwards and backwards from any number to 120; count in 3s from multiples of 3. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 2)
- Odd and even numbers to 120 (last digit 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 = even).
- Numbers in te reo Māori to 100.

### Operations
- Addition and subtraction facts to 20 (17 + 3). ✓ (Sums, Number bonds)
- Derive facts from known ones (7 + 2 = 9 so 70 + 20 = 90).
- Add and subtract within 100 (32 + 20, 32 + 2). ✓ (Sums)
- Doubles and halves to 20. ✓ (Doubles and halves, Year 2)
- Add three one-digit numbers (7 + 3 + 6).
- Add 100 to a one-digit number.
- One-step + and − word problems to 100; two-step to 20. ✓ (Story sums, Year 2)
- Link skip counting to the 2, 5 and 10 times tables; learn those tables and their division facts. ✓ (Times bonds, Times grid)
- Multiply and divide with answers up to 100. ✓ (Sums, Arrays)
- Knows: × 1 and ÷ 1 change nothing; × 0 is 0; order doesn't matter for × but does for ÷; + and − undo each other, and so do × and ÷.

### Fractions and money
- Halves, thirds and quarters (½, ⅓, ⅔, ¼, ²⁄₄, ¾) of sets, amounts and shapes, in words and symbols. ✓ (Fractions)
- ²⁄₄ is the same as ½. ✓ (Fractions accepts equal fractions)
- Compare two fractions (halves are bigger than thirds, bigger than quarters).
- Find a half, third or quarter of a set by seeing groups, not sharing one by one.
- Find the whole from a part (if ½ is 5, the whole is 10).
- Order NZ coins and notes by value; group like coins and total them. ✓ partly (Money, Year 2: totals)
- Make an amount using only coins or only notes. ✓ partly (Money, Year 2: totals, not making)

### Algebra
- True or false comparisons to 120 (16 > 60?), using >, < and =.
- True or false and open number sentences with +, −, × and ÷ (18 + __ = 17 + 6, 6 ÷ __ = 2, 2 + 2 + 2 = 3 × 2?). ✓ partly (Missing number)
- Find the unit of repeat in a pattern and use it to predict what comes later (ACDC in ACDCACDC...).

### Measurement
- Measure length, weight or capacity with an informal unit used again and again (paper clips). ✋ ✓ partly (Rulers, Year 1: blocks)
- Compare and order several objects by those units.
- Estimate and measure in cm, g and mL with a marked tool (ruler, scale, jug). ✓ partly (Rulers, Year 2: cm only)
- Perimeter of a polygon in metric units. ✓ (Area: Perimeter)
- Full, half, quarter and three-quarter turns, clockwise and anticlockwise.
- Months and seasons in order; the month before and after; ordinals for months.
- Describe how long familiar things take.
- Tell the time to the hour, half hour and quarter hour on analogue and digital clocks, saying "past" and "o'clock" only ("half past", "quarter past"). Knows: 60 minutes in an hour, 30 in half an hour. ✓ partly (Clocks, Year 2: analogue only). Out of step: Clocks Year 2 also asks "quarter to", which is Year 3.

### Geometry
- Name and sort 2D and 3D shapes by attributes: oval, semicircle, pentagon, hexagon, cuboid, pyramid, cone. Words: edge, face, vertex. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 2: sides, corners, faces; not naming)
- Flip, slide and turn 2D shapes to make a pattern or a shape.
- Give and follow directions with steps and half and quarter turns ✋ (could be a grid game).
- Read diagrams to say where things are relative to each other.

### Statistics
- Collect category data (favourite pets) ✋, sort it, decide if "other" is needed, tally it.
- Make picture graphs and dot plots.
- Describe them and answer most and least questions. ✓ partly (Graphs, Year 2: tally charts and picture graphs, how many and how many more; no dot plots until Year 4, no most/least)

## Year 3

### Number
- Read, write and show numbers to 1,000 with base-ten blocks. ✓ (Place value: Blocks)
- Compare and order numbers to 1,000.
- Place value of each digit in three-digit numbers. ✓ (Place value)
- Count collections beyond 120 by grouping in 10s and 100s.
- Round to the nearest 10 or 100; estimate an answer before working it out.
- Count forwards and backwards in 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s and 8s from multiples (8, 16, 24, 32). ✓ (Number patterns, Year 3)
- Count in 10s and 100s from any number to 1,000. ✓ (Place value: 10 more / 10 less, Number lines)

### Operations
- Complements to 100 (34 + __ = 100).
- Add and subtract within 1,000 (329 + 3, 329 + 80, 329 − 200, 137 + 54), including column methods with regrouping. ✓ (Sums)
- Derive facts with place value (7 + 2 = 9 so 700 + 200 = 900).
- One-step + and − word problems to 1,000; two-step to 100. ✓ (Story sums, Year 3)
- Times tables and division facts for 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10. ✓ (Times bonds, Times grid)
- Multiply and divide by sharing, grouping, repeated adding or known facts. ✓ (Arrays)
- Multiply a one- or two-digit number by a one-digit number (4 × 6, 2 × 23). ✓ (Sums)
- Divide by a one-digit number, no remainders (24 ÷ 3, 32 ÷ 4), from known facts. ✓ (Times and divide, printed across: 24 ÷ 3 = ___). The original Sums tile keeps the long-division layout, a Year 5 written method.
- Knows: dividing by zero is impossible.

### Fractions and money
- Read, write and show fractions of sets, amounts, shapes and on a number line, small denominators. ✓ (Fractions)
- Count in unit fractions up to 1 (⅕, ⅖, ⅗...).
- Compare unit fractions with denominators to 12 (bigger denominator = smaller piece: 1⁄12 < ⅙).
- Compare fractions with the same denominator (⅜ < ⅝).
- Spot equal fractions with pictures (³⁄₆ = ½; ²⁄₂ = ³⁄₃ = 1). ✓ (Fractions accepts them)
- Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator within one (⅛ + ²⁄₈ + ³⁄₈ = ⁶⁄₈).
- Unit fraction of a number by dividing (⅓ of 15 = 15 ÷ 3).
- Whole from a unit fraction (¼ of a set is 3, so the set is 12).
- Write money as dollars and cents without a decimal point ($2 and 50 cents). ✓ (Money, Year 3)
- Make amounts with $1 and $2 coins and $5, $10, $20, $50 and $100 notes. ✓ partly (Money: totals, not making)
- Give change by adding and subtracting. ✓ (Money: Change)

### Algebra
- True or false comparisons to 1,000 (313 < 330?).
- True or false and open number sentences with all four operations (217 − __ = 105, 12 ÷ 3 = 5 − 2?). ✓ partly (Missing number)
- Recognise, continue and make growing number patterns (a rule that adds or takes away each time). ✓ partly (Number patterns, Year 3: continue, not make)

### Measurement
- Estimate and measure in cm and m, g and kg, mL and L; compare and order by those units. ✓ partly (Rulers, Year 3: cm, not from 0)
- Perimeter of polygons. ✓ (Area: Perimeter)
- Area of rectangles by counting equal squares. ✓ (Area: Count squares)
- Full, half, quarter and three-quarter turns, clockwise and anticlockwise.
- How long events take, in seconds up to years.
- Tell the time to the nearest 5 minutes and nearest minute on analogue and digital clocks, minutes past and minutes to ("10 past", "quarter to", a.m. and p.m.). ✓ partly (Clocks, Years 3 and 4: analogue only; not a.m. and p.m.)
- Compare units of time. Knows: 15 minutes in a quarter hour, 60 seconds in a minute, 24 hours in a day, 52 weeks and 365 days in a year (366 in a leap year, every 4 years), months about 4 weeks with different numbers of days, decade 10 years, century 100, millennium 1,000.

### Geometry
- Name, picture and sort regular polygons up to 10 sides (all sides and angles equal). Words: right angle, horizontal, vertical. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 3: count sides up to 10)
- Find lines of symmetry; complete a symmetrical pattern or picture. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 3: how many lines)
- Step-by-step directions with half and quarter turns and distances; north, east, south, west.
- Use simple maps to find things relative to other things.

### Statistics
- Collect and sort category data ✋; collect counts (teeth lost, shoe sizes) ✋.
- Make dot plots and bar graphs.
- Describe them and answer questions, including ones that need sums (how many teeth did the class lose altogether?). ✓ (Graphs, Year 3: bar graphs, how many more, altogether)

## Year 4

### Number
- Read, write, compare and order numbers to 10,000 with base-ten structure. Knows: each place is 10 times the one on its right; 0 holds a place. ✓ (Place value: Up to 9,999, thousands as cubes; More or less, Year 4: compare; not ordering three or more)
- Round to the nearest 10, 100 or 1,000; round tenths to the nearest whole. ✓ (Rounding, Year 4: number line or just numbers)
- Count forwards and backwards in 2s to 9s, 25s and 50s from multiples. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 4; Number lines: 25s, 50s)
- Count in 10s, 100s and 1,000s from any number to 10,000. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 4; Place value: 1,000 more / less; Number lines: 1000s)

### Operations
- Add and subtract up to four-digit numbers, mentally or in columns. ✓ (Sums, 1000–9999)
- All times tables and division facts, 2 to 10. ✓ (Times bonds, Times grid)
- Multiply and divide mentally with place value and known facts, including × 0, × 1 and ÷ 1. ✓ partly (True or false, Year 4: × 10 and ÷ 10)
- Multiply a two- or three-digit number by a one-digit number. ✓ (Sums)
- Divide up to a three-digit number by a one-digit number, no remainders (65 ÷ 5). ✓ (Times and divide, printed across: 65 ÷ 5 = ___). The original Sums tile keeps the long-division layout, a Year 5 written method (column and area-model multiplying do start in Year 4).
- Scale with × and ÷ (double or halve a recipe). ✓ (Story sums, Year 4: recipe stories, double, halve, × 3, × 4)

### Fractions, decimals and money
- Tenths as fractions and decimals (³⁄₁₀ = 0.3); compare and order them. ✓ (Decimals, Year 4: bars, number line, ³⁄₁₀ = __ . __; More or less, Fractions kind: ³⁄₁₀ ☐ 0.5; ordering not built)
- Know ½ = 0.5 and other tenths as decimals. ✓ (Decimals, Year 4: Picture kind)
- Divide one- and two-digit numbers by 10 to make tenths (7 ÷ 10 = 0.7); multiply tenths by 10. ✓ (Decimals, Year 4: × and ÷ by 10)
- Compare and order fractions with the same top or same bottom number. ✓ partly (More or less, Fractions kind: comparing; ordering not built)
- Place fractions, improper fractions and mixed numbers on a number line (⁹⁄₈ = 1⅛). ✓ partly (Fractions, Past 1: improper fractions on a 0-to-2 line and two shapes; mixed numbers need more than digits)
- Spot equal fractions with pictures. ✓ (Fractions)
- Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, past one whole (³⁄₈ + ³⁄₈ + ³⁄₈ = ⁹⁄₈ = 1⅛). ✓ (Fractions, Add and take away kind; answered as ⁹⁄₈)
- Add and subtract decimals to one place (1.3 + 0.2 = 1.5). ✓ (Decimals, Year 4: Add and take away)
- Unit fraction of a number (⅓ of 300); the whole from a unit fraction (¼ of a set is 7, so the set is 28). ✓ (Fractions of a number, Year 4)
- Total cost of several items in whole dollars, or many of one item, and the change. ✓ (Money, Year 4: Change kind)
- Make an amount in different ways ($5 and 80 cents). ✓ (Money, Year 4: Make it, tap coins and notes into a tray; any way that adds up)

### Algebra
- True or false and open number sentences with + and − (8205 − 4721 = 3484?; 4200 − __ = 4001). ✓ (True or false, Year 4; Balance, Year 4; Missing number)
- The same with × and ÷ (11 × 7 = 78?; __ ÷ 10 = 12). ✓ (True or false, Year 4; Balance, Year 4; Missing number)
- Knows: doing the same thing to both sides keeps it balanced. ✓ (Balance, Year 4: 36 + 17 = ☐ + 20, 6 × 8 = ☐ × 4)
- Growing patterns that add, take away or multiply by the same number each time (5, 7, 9, 11...; 3, 6, 12, 24...): continue, make and describe them.

### Measurement
- Make estimation benchmarks from familiar things (body parts, the walk to school). ✋
- Measure in mixed units with the right tool (1 m and 23 cm, 2 L and 500 mL). ✋ partly
- Temperature in degrees Celsius (a thermometer picture works). ✓ (Rulers, Year 4: Thermometer)
- Perimeter in mm, cm and m. ✓ (Area: Perimeter)
- Area of irregular shapes from squares and half squares. ✓ (Area: Odd shapes)
- Area of rectangles by multiplying the sides. ✓ (Area: Area, Missing side)
- Volume of cuboids by filling with cubes. ✓ (Area: Cubes, counting a drawn cuboid)
- Estimate angles against 90°, 180° and 360°. Knows: a full turn is 360°, a half turn 180°, a quarter turn 90°; rectangles have four right angles. ✓ (Angles, Year 4: smaller than, a right angle, or bigger; quarter, half and full turns in degrees)
- Tell the time to the nearest minute, analogue and digital. ✓ (Clocks, Year 4)
- Durations in mixed units (1 h 42 min, 3 min 21 s).
- Change between time units (3 weeks = 21 days, 90 seconds = 1½ minutes, 48 hours = 2 days). ✓ (Change units, Year 4: weeks, days, hours, minutes, seconds, months, years; mixed as __ h __ min and __ min __ s)

### Geometry
- Classify regular and irregular polygons up to 12 sides by edges, vertices and angles. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 4: count sides and right angles). The words "quadrilateral" and "parallel line" are taught, but finding parallel lines is Year 5 and the named quadrilaterals are Year 6.
- Count lines of symmetry in 2D shapes (a circle has infinitely many). ✓ (Shapes, Year 4)
- Match 3D shapes to 2D drawings and views from different sides.
- One-step reflections, translations and rotations of 2D shapes. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 4: Moves, say which move it was: flip, slide or turn; not drawing the move)
- Grid references (A1, B2) to find squares and plot positions on a map. ✓ (Map, Year 4: tap a square from its reference, or pick the reference of a shape)

### Statistics
- Collect numerical data, rounding if needed (skips in 30 seconds) ✋.
- Make dot plots and bar graphs with a title, labels, units and a scale from 0.
- Read dot plots: the frequency of a value, individual values, the shape, the middle, the spread (lowest to highest). Tell "the value" from "how many had that value" apart (the most pets anyone has vs the most common number of pets). ✓ (Graphs, Year 4: frequency, most common, most and fewest anyone has, spread; not the shape or the middle)

## Year 5

Year 5 buttons so far (C1, C2a, C2b, C3a, C3b, C3c, 2026-11-17): Add and take away, Times and divide, Number patterns, True or false, More or less, Balance, Rounding, Decimals, Fractions of a number, Money, Clocks, Change units, Angles, Map, Shapes; Area has three Year 5 kinds (L-shapes, Same area, Cube layers); Place value goes up to 999,999. Other ticks are activities whose own settings happen to reach.

### Number
- Read, write, compare and order numbers to 1,000,000. ✓ (Place value up to 999,999: expanded form and 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 more or less; More or less, Year 5: comparing six-digit numbers; not ordering)
- Factor pairs of any product up to 10 × 10 (3 and 4 are a factor pair of 12).
- Round to the nearest 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 or 100,000; round tenths and hundredths to the nearest whole. ✓ (Rounding, Year 5: six digits to the nearest 1,000, 10,000, 100,000; tenths and hundredths to the whole; Year 4 has 10 and 100)
- Count in 11s and 12s from multiples; in 1,000s, 10,000s and 100,000s from any number to 100,000. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 5)
- Count backwards through 0 into negative numbers. ✓ (Number patterns, Year 5: "6, 4, 2, 0, ☐" with the minus printed before the line; she writes the size) Knows: negatives sit left of 0 (or below it on an upright line); 0 is neither; they turn up as debt and temperature.

### Operations
- Add and subtract bigger and bigger whole numbers. ✓ (Add and take away, Year 5: five and six digits, stacked)
- All times tables and division facts, 2 to 12. ✓ (Times bonds, Times grid, Times and divide Year 5)
- Multiply and divide bigger numbers mentally with facts, factor pairs and multiples.
- Multiply a three- or four-digit number by one digit; multiply two two-digit numbers. ✓ (Times and divide, Year 5: four digits × one digit, two digits × two digits, stacked with room for working)
- Divide up to a four-digit number by one digit, **with a remainder** (278 ÷ 4 = 69 remainder 2), including the written method. ✓ (Times and divide, Year 5: the long-division bracket, answered "__ r __"; about one in four exact)

### Fractions, decimals, percentages and money
- Tenths and hundredths as fractions and decimals; compare and order numbers to two decimal places (0.12 < 0.2). ✓ (Decimals, Year 5: hundred squares, a tenth cut into hundredths on a line, ³⁷⁄₁₀₀ = __ . __; More or less and True or false, Year 5: compare to two places, 0.5 = 0.50; ordering not built)
- Know ½, ¼ and ¾ as decimals, and fractions out of 10 or 100. ✓ (Decimals, Year 5: Picture kind, ¼ = __ . __, ³⁷⁄₁₀₀)
- 10%, 25% and 50% as fractions and decimals. ✓ partly (Fractions of a number, Year 5: Percent on the bar model, 4 boxes for 25%, 10 for 10%; not written as decimals)
- Divide one- and two-digit numbers by 10 or 100; multiply decimals by 10 and 100. ✓ (Decimals, Year 5: × and ÷ by 10 and 100; up to three digits ÷)
- Compare fractions where one bottom number is a multiple of the other; families of equal fractions; mixed numbers and improper fractions that are equal. ✓ partly (More or less and True or false, Year 5: 2/3 and 5/9, 2/3 = 6/9; Fractions accepts equal fractions; not mixed numbers)
- Add and subtract fractions with the same bottom number, or where one is a multiple of the other (⅔ + ¹⁄₉ = ⁷⁄₉). ✓ (Fractions: Add and take away, and Add different, pictures cut to the bigger bottom number)
- Add and subtract decimals to two places (1.31 + 0.22 = 1.53). ✓ (Add and take away, Year 5: about a third of cards, "__ . __"; Decimals, Year 5, also a tenth with a hundredth, 0.4 + 0.25)
- A non-unit fraction of a number (⅔ of 24); the whole from a part (8 is ⅖ of a set). ✓ (Fractions of a number, Year 5: a second brace under the n boxes)
- 10%, 25% and 50% of a number; the whole when given 25% or 50%. ✓ (Fractions of a number, Year 5: Percent)
- Total cost in dollars and cents, change from the nearest $10; money written with a decimal point; round to the nearest dollar. ✓ (Money, Year 5: price tags like $3.45, total or change from the next note up, "$ __ . __"; Rounding, Year 5: $48.65 to the nearest dollar)

### Algebra
- Fill in <, > or = between two calculations (2,456 × 2 __ 1,228 × 4). ✓ (More or less, Year 5, Kind Sums: halve one and double the other, give and take)
- True or false and open number sentences with big numbers (999,999 − __ = 899,999). ✓ (True or false, Year 5; Balance, Year 5: six-digit place changes, × and ÷ by 10, 100, 1,000, both sides to work out; Missing number)
- Growing patterns that change by a constant amount, decimals included (3, 4.5, 6, 7.5...). ✓ (Number patterns, Year 5: decimal steps in tenths, "☐ = __ . __")

### Measurement
- Measure accurately with a ruler, scales, jug, thermometer and timer, in mixed units (2 hours and 30 minutes). ✋ partly
- Change between m and cm. Knows: 10 mm in a cm, 100 cm in a m, 1,000 mm in a m, 1,000 m in a km. ✓ (Change units, Year 5: m and cm with decimals, 2 m 35 cm = __ . __ m, 1.8 m = ___ cm; km and m; cm and mm)
- Area of irregular shapes from squares, half squares and part squares (roughly).
- Area of rectangles by multiplying the sides. ✓ (Area)
- Volume of a cuboid: cubes in a layer × number of layers. ✓ (Area: Cube layers, Year 5, up to 5 × 4 × 4 with the layers drawn apart)
- Perimeter of regular polygons and other straight-sided shapes. ✓ partly (Area: Perimeter on rectangles, and L-shapes (Year 5): L, step and T shapes with every side labelled; not regular polygons)
- Same area, different perimeter (and the other way round). ✓ partly (Area: Same area (Year 5), tap the rectangle with the same area and a different perimeter; not the other way round)
- Name angles: acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex; measure and draw angles to 180° with a protractor. ✓ partly (Angles, Year 5: Name, tap the word; Measure, a protractor drawn over the angle, multiples of 5; not drawing)
- Tell the time on analogue and digital clocks; how long between two times, with a.m. and p.m. and 24-hour time. ✓ (Clocks, Year 5: a face or words with the part of the day, written in 24-hour time; How long?, two times in a.m. / p.m. or 24-hour, "__ h __ min")

### Geometry
- Describe prisms by their cross section, faces, edges and vertices. ✓ partly (Shapes, Year 5: faces, edges, corners of triangular, pentagonal and hexagonal prisms; not naming the cross section)
- Find parallel and perpendicular lines, including the sides of polygons. ✓ (Shapes, Year 5: "How many pairs of parallel sides?" and "How many right angles?" on quadrilaterals and hexagons; tap "Which two lines are parallel?" / "perpendicular?")
- Match 3D shapes to their nets. ✓ (Shapes, Year 5, Nets: cube, cuboid, triangular prism, square pyramid, cylinder; three nets, one right)
- Say which transformation was done (reflection, translation, rotation).
- Read and make grid maps with grid references and north, east, south, west. ✓ partly (Map, Year 5: "Start at B2. Go 2 north, 3 east", tap where you finish; reading only, not making)

### Statistics
- Collect measured data with a set rounding rule ✋; collect paired category data (what we do at lunch, and gender) ✋.
- Tables that group measured data into ranges (0–0.99, 1–1.99...). ✓ (Graphs, Year 5: long jump and throw in four bands, how many less than, or at least, a cut, and altogether; reading only; C4a)
- Clustered bar graphs for paired categories (two colours side by side, with a key), and questions about them. ✓ (Graphs, Year 5: three days, two colours with a key, how many more on one day, one colour altogether, tap the day with more; C4a)

### Probability
- Run a chance game many times and describe the outcomes ✋ (a screen can run the trials). ✓ partly (Chance, Year 5, Try it: "Pick 20" really picks 20 times from the bag, a tally fills, then "Which came up most?"; C4b)
- Call an everyday event impossible, unlikely, even chance, likely or certain. ✓ (Chance, Year 5, Words: picking a colour from a bag of counters, five word tiles; bags, not everyday events; C4b)
- Put events on a 0-to-1 line by how likely they are. ✓ (Chance, Year 5, 0 to 1 line: five marks to tap, 0, between, ½, between, 1; C4b)

## Year 6

### Number
- Read, write, compare and order any whole number.
- Factor pairs of any product up to 12 × 12.
- Round to the nearest 10 up to the nearest million; round hundredths to the nearest whole or tenth.
- Square and cube numbers and the ² and ³ signs. Knows: squares to 144, cubes to 125.
- Count forwards and backwards with negative numbers (from −6, back in 2s).

### Operations
- Order of operations: brackets, then squaring, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right.
- Add and subtract any whole numbers.
- Multiply any whole number by a two-digit number (542 × 12).
- Divide up to a five-digit number by one digit, with a remainder (1283 ÷ 5 = 256 remainder 3).
- Write the remainder as a whole number, a fraction or a rounded decimal, whichever suits the story (⅙ of 31 = 31 ÷ 6 = 5⅙).

### Fractions, decimals, percentages and money
- Tenths, hundredths and thousandths; compare and order numbers to three decimal places.
- Know ½, ¼, ¾, ⅕, ⅖, ⅗, ⅘ as decimals and percentages; change decimals to fractions and percentages (0.31 = ³¹⁄₁₀₀ = 31%).
- Multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1,000 (1.3 × 10 = 13).
- Find equal fractions and simplify them; compare and order fractions where one bottom number is a multiple of all the others; change between mixed numbers and improper fractions.
- Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers where one bottom number is a multiple of the other.
- Add and subtract decimals to three places.
- A non-unit fraction of a number (⅔ of 240); the whole from a non-unit fraction (¾ of a set is 90).
- 1%, 10%, 20%, 25%, 50% and 75% of a number; the whole from a percentage (75% is 24).
- Compare two amounts and find missing values with fractions, decimals and percentages.
- 10%, 25% and 50% of whole dollar amounts (50% of $280); is there enough money?

### Algebra
- True or false and open number sentences with all four operations, ≤ and ≥, and the order of operations (8 × 7 ≤ 8 × 5 + 4²?).
- Put a growing pattern's rule into words and predict later terms.
- Make a table of values from a rule and plot it as (x, y) points, including points on the axes.

### Measurement
- Estimate length, weight, capacity, temperature and time from benchmarks. ✋
- Change between m and cm, g and kg, L and mL, joining mixed units into decimals (10 kg and 500 g = 10.5 kg). Knows: milli = a thousandth, centi = a hundredth, kilo = a thousand.
- Area of rectangles and right-angled triangles (half the rectangle), in cm² and m².
- Volume of cubes and cuboids by multiplying, in cm³ and m³.
- Measure and draw angles to 360° with a protractor.
- Find unknown angles: angles at a point add to 360°, on a straight line to 180°, vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Change between hours, minutes and seconds; durations in 12- and 24-hour time; minutes across an hour (2:53 pm to 3:28 pm).
- Read timetables (bus, train) to find how long things take. Knows: solar calendars (Gregorian) and lunar ones (maramataka Māori, Chinese).

### Geometry
- Sort and compare 2D shapes, including kinds of triangle and the quadrilaterals: trapezium (one pair of parallel sides), parallelogram (two pairs), kite (two pairs of equal sides next to each other), rectangle (four right angles), rhombus (four equal sides), square (both). Compare prisms and pyramids.
- Interior angles of triangles and quadrilaterals.
- Rotational symmetry and its order (a circle's is infinite).
- Make and describe tessellations with rotation, reflection and translation.
- Predict the result of two-step transformations.
- Maps with grid references, a simple scale, compass points, turns in degrees and distances in m and km.

### Statistics
- Collect time-series data (the weight of a kilogram of carrots over 5 days) ✋.
- Work out the mean and the range.
- Make time-series (line) graphs; choose the right graph for the data.
- Say whether a time-series graph shows a trend; read graphs from the news.

### Probability
- List the sample space (a die: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6).
- Work out the chance of one outcome, and of a spinner colour as a fraction.
- Chances of combined outcomes; check that all the chances add to 1.
