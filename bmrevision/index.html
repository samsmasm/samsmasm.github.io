2. Topic cards

One card per topic, reds first. Use at least two businesses from the business bank, including one social enterprise or non-profit.

  1. Define it: a 2-mark definition.
  2. Apply it: one advantage and one disadvantage, using a business. For a calculation topic, invent data, calculate, and say what the answer means.
  3. Connect it: how it affects another unit.
  4. Tool it: use one tool from the toolkit grid. What does it show, and what does it miss?
  5. Exam it: write an exam question on it, then your conclusion with one limitation.

3. KOTO case

KOTO ("Know One, Teach One") began as a sandwich shop in Hanoi in 1999 and was Vietnam's first social enterprise. Founded by Jimmy Pham, it is a not-for-profit that gives at-risk and disadvantaged young people 24 months of hospitality and life-skills training.

Its income comes from training restaurants, catering, cooking classes, donors and partners. In 2026 it began a five-year partnership with the Australian Government called Our Turn. Its Ho Chi Minh City restaurant, ST25 by KOTO, was selected in the MICHELIN Guide 2026. More: koto.com.au

The decisions and figures in these tasks are invented for practice. They are not KOTO's real plans.

Decision tree 1: grow in Ho Chi Minh City

KOTO wants to train more young people in Ho Chi Minh City. Its options:

  1. Option 1: open a second training restaurant in Thu Duc. Cost: $250,000.
  2. Option 2: expand catering with a larger kitchen and two vans. Cost: $120,000.
  3. Option 3: no change. Cost: $0.

The tree is started for you. Copy it onto paper and complete it, then check your numbers.

Decision tree 2: new income for training

KOTO wants a new source of income to pay for more trainee places. Its options:

  1. Option 1: run cooking classes for tourists. Cost: $40,000.
  2. Option 2: open a café kiosk in an office building. Cost: $80,000.

Draw a fully labelled decision tree from scratch on paper, then check your numbers.

Tools and recommendation

Use decision tree 1 (Option 1 or Option 2) and this extra information:

  • Thu Duc has many new offices and universities, but rents are rising.
  • Demand for event catering grows each year, but large hotels now compete for it.
  • Trainers say trainees learn more skills in a restaurant than in a catering kitchen.
  • Many donors prefer to fund training rather than buildings.
  1. Apply two more tools from the toolkit grid, including one situational tool.
  2. Rank all your tools by how useful they are for this decision, one sentence each.
  3. Write one PEEL paragraph, as part of a 10-mark answer to: "Discuss whether KOTO should choose Option 1 or Option 2." Use your tool results as evidence, explain why they matter, and consider the trainee places.
