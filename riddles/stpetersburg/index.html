The riddle

A casino flips a coin until it lands heads. The prize starts at $2 and doubles every time the coin lands tails.

How did the average win behave as more games were played?

The answer

The average prize is infinite. Yet half of all games pay just $2, and almost nobody would pay more than about $20.

    Show the calculation
    The average prize adds up chance x prize for every row: Heads on flip 1: 1/2 x $2 = $1 Heads on flip 2: 1/4 x $4 = $1 Heads on flip 3: 1/8 x $8 = $1 Heads on flip 4: 1/16 x $16 = $1 ... Every row adds another $1, and the rows never end. So the average prize is $1 + $1 + $1 + ... = infinite. But a real casino cannot pay more than it has. If it can pay at most $1 million (about 2 to the power 20), only about 20 rows count, so a fair price is about $21.

    Give the casino a limit

    No casino has endless money. If prizes stop growing at the casino's limit, the average prize becomes a normal number.
