The riddle

A casino flips a coin until it lands heads. The prize starts at $2 and doubles every time the coin lands tails.

The answer

There is no number. The more games you play, the higher your average win climbs, with no limit. Mathematicians call this long-run average the expected value, and here it is infinite.

    Show the calculation
    The average prize adds up chance x prize for every row: Heads on flip 1: 1/2 x $2 = $1 Heads on flip 2: 1/4 x $4 = $1 Heads on flip 3: 1/8 x $8 = $1 Heads on flip 4: 1/16 x $16 = $1 ... Every row adds another $1, and the rows never end. So the average prize is $1 + $1 + $1 + ... = infinite. But a real casino cannot pay more than it has. If it can pay at most $1 million (about 2 to the power 20), only about 20 rows count, so the expected value is about $21. That is the average prize, not the right price to pay. What a game is worth to you depends on how you feel about risk.

    Give the casino a limit

    No casino has endless money. If prizes stop growing at the casino's limit, the average prize becomes a normal number. Run the games a few times: the average jumps around, because rare huge wins still decide it.
