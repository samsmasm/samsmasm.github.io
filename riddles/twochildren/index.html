The riddle

A family has two children. Each child is equally likely to be a boy or a girl.

You are told one fact: at least one of the two is a boy.

What is the chance that both children are boys?

Your guess

The chance both are boys, knowing at least one is.

What do you see?

Among the families with at least one boy, how many had two boys?

The answer

1 in 3

About 33%. But if you are told the older child is a boy, it becomes 1 in 2.

    Show the calculation
    Two children can come in four ways, each equally likely: older younger boy boy 1 in 4 boy girl 1 in 4 girl boy 1 in 4 girl girl 1 in 4 "At least one is a boy" rules out only girl, girl. Three ways are left, and one of them is boy, boy: 1 in 3. "The older one is a boy" also rules out girl, boy. Two ways are left, and one of them is boy, boy: 1 in 2.
