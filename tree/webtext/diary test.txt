The streets are broad, and the buildings handsome and substantial, such as any town in England would be proud of, and which would be considered utterly out of keeping in a place with only forty thousand inhabitants. The Grand Hotel in which we are staying seems to have every modern appliance and it would not take a second place even in London. An extraordinary tram line starts near the door, upon which we made a journey soon after we arrived. It works with an endless wire rope and goes up High Street, which is exceedingly steep. We travelled about a mile and a half upward very pleasantly, but coming down is trying for weak nerves, we took the front seat and had the tremendous slope straight before and below us. They only charge

Called upon Mr Morris (introduced by [K Turnbull]), agent of a Land Co. He says plenty of farms can be bought but to do any good a man must know the trade as land now sells at a fair value, and cannot be got for next to nothing as in the old days.
31st. Called upon Mr [Mackie], Agent of the Bk of NZ, also upon Mr Denniston, a friend of Mr Kerr, who took Wilson and I to see the Mosgiel Woollen Factory's warehouse. We saw very well made tweeds, flannels, blankets, rugs and stockings. Bought a rug. Called upon Mr [Leitheard?], Commissioner of Crown Lands, an old friend of A Hepburn, afterwards had lunch at his house.
