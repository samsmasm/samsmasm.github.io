# IB Business Management: Structured Question Guide

Reference for generating well-calibrated IB BM exam-style questions. Use this when writing or checking questions for any IB BM context.

---

## Assessment Objectives

| AO | Label | What it means |
|----|-------|---------------|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding | Recall and define. No application to context needed or rewarded. |
| AO2 | Application and analysis | Break a concept down and apply it to the specific case or context. Explain the mechanism. |
| AO3 | Synthesis and evaluation | Two-sided analysis with a supported conclusion or judgment. |

AOs are about cognitive demand. Multiple command terms map to the same AO.

---

## Command Terms by AO

**AO1:** Define, Identify, List, Outline, State

**AO2:** Analyse, Apply, Comment, Demonstrate, Distinguish, Explain, Suggest

**AO3:** Compare, Compare and contrast, Contrast, Discuss, Evaluate, Examine, Justify, Recommend, To what extent

---

## Mark Allocations

### 2 marks — AO1

**Command terms:** Define, Identify, State, List

**Identify / State / List:** 1 mark per discrete item. "Identify two stakeholders" = 2 marks = 1 mark each. No partial credit for vague answers.

**Define:** 1 mark for the core definition + 1 mark for development (an example, a qualification, or an additional clause that sharpens the definition).

**No case application needed or rewarded at this level.** A student should be able to answer a 2-mark question from knowledge alone.

---

### 4 marks — AO2

**Command terms:** Explain (one point), Suggest, Distinguish

**Structure:** Point (2 marks) + explanation of mechanism applied to the case (2 marks).

The key test: does the student need to explain WHY or HOW? If the question can be answered by naming or classifying something, it is AO1, not AO2.

- "Explain one reason why BVG chose acquisition over organic growth" = AO2 (requires mechanism + application)
- "Classify BVG's growth as internal or external" = AO1 (requires only identification)

---

### 6 marks — AO2 breadth or AO3

Two distinct versions:

**(a) AO2 breadth — Explain two points**

Command term: Explain (two points)

Structure: 3 marks per point = identify the point (1) + explain the mechanism (1) + apply to the specific case (1).

**(b) AO3 introductory — Examine, Analyse, Discuss**

Command terms: Examine, Analyse, Discuss

Structure: Arguments on both sides (roughly 3 marks each) + a conclusion or supported judgment.

Do not use AO3 command terms for a question that only has one valid side. "Examine the view that X" implies the student must consider arguments for and against, then reach a conclusion.

---

### 10 marks — AO3 extended

**Command terms:** Evaluate, To what extent, Justify, Recommend

**Structure:** Well-developed arguments on both sides + a supported conclusion. Marked in bands, not point-by-point. Top marks require a clear, defended judgment, not just a list of pros and cons.

---

## Calibration Checklist

Before finalising any question, ask:

1. **Does the command term match the AO?** "Explain" should require explaining a mechanism, not just naming something.
2. **Does the mark allocation match the scope?** A 4-mark question needs one well-explained point. A 2-mark question needs one item or a definition.
3. **Is application needed?** AO1 questions should not require case knowledge. AO2 and AO3 questions should be impossible to answer well without engaging with the specific context.
4. **For AO3: are there genuinely two sides?** If one side is obviously stronger, the question may not reward evaluative thinking.

---

## Common Traps

- Writing a classification task as a 4-mark "explain" question. Classification = AO1.
- Setting "define" as a 4-mark question. Define is almost always 2 marks.
- Using "evaluate" for a question with only one plausible answer. Evaluation requires genuine tension.
- Writing an "identify two" question as a 2+2 developed answer. It is 1 mark per item: two brief, distinct answers.

## Example Questions by Mark Level

| Marks | Command term | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 2 | Define | Define the term *private equity*. [2] |
| 2 | Identify | Identify two external stakeholders of Brand Velocity Group. [2] |
| 4 | Explain | Explain one reason why BVG chose to grow through acquisition rather than organically. [4] |
| 4 | Suggest | Suggest one reason why celebrity involvement may not reduce business risk for BVG. [4] |
| 6 | Explain (two) | Explain two external sources of finance BVG could have used to fund the RCX acquisition. [6] |
| 6 | Examine | Examine the view that private equity ownership of youth sports prioritises investor returns over participant welfare. [6] |
| 10 | To what extent | To what extent does Eli Manning's personal brand protect Brand Velocity Group from the business risks it faces? [10] |
