    Notes

    • Each day shows its peak hour: the hour with the highest wet bulb across the weather models, in the city's local time. Temperature, humidity and feels like are taken at that hour. Every ranged figure shows the lowest and highest value across the sources.
    • Peak sun is the day's strongest sunlight in watts per square metre, and UV max is the day's highest UV index. Not every model reports UV, so it can be blank.
    • Feels like comes from the weather model. If you edit a temperature or humidity, it switches to a simple shade estimate with light wind.
    • Stull (2011) uses temperature and humidity only and is good to about 1°C in normal conditions. The full method solves the psychrometric equation and also uses pressure.
    • Weather models describe a grid cell, not a single street, and an airport observation can differ from the cell around it.
    • 35°C is the theoretical limit for people resting in shade. Lab studies on healthy young adults find strain starting lower, around 30 to 31°C in humid heat.
    • This is plain wet bulb temperature. WBGT, used for outdoor work and sport, also counts sun and wind.
    • The forecast detail compares up to eight weather models: ECMWF, GFS, ICON, GEM, JMA, Meteo-France, UK Met Office and CMA. Today is hourly, tomorrow is in three-hour blocks and the day after is in six-hour blocks. Hours already gone today are the models’ own estimate, not station readings.
    • Rain is shown model by model, not only averaged, because models often disagree on whether a storm happens and when. Bar height is the average rate and each colour is one model’s share, so a long drizzle and a short downpour look different.
    • Heat level uses simple thresholds I chose (feels like 32, 38 and 45°C, wet bulb 25, 28 and 31°C), not an official scale. The best and hardest three hours between 6am and 6pm score feels like temperature plus a penalty for rain and UV.
    • Of the weather models only GFS publishes a UV index, so UV is GFS plus CAMS, the European atmosphere service’s UV forecast. Some models also skip other things (JMA has no gusts, sunshine or storm energy); a missing value is left out, never counted as zero.
    • Air quality comes from Open-Meteo’s separate air quality service and is one forecast, not a comparison of models. Storm energy (CAPE) shows how much fuel there is for thunderstorms, not whether one will form.
    • Example values are rough mid-October guesses. The spread between example sources, the day-to-day changes, sun, UV, feels like and everything in the forecast detail (all eight models, rain, wind, air quality) are simulated. Live data, when it loads, comes from Open-Meteo.com under CC BY 4.0.
