Business Q1 / 10

A pilotless electric flying taxi recently carried a passenger in Japan's Yamanashi Prefecture — no pilot, vertical takeoff, fully autonomous. Who actually built the aircraft?

a A Japanese startup backed by Toyota
b A Chinese manufacturer
c Boeing's urban air mobility division

b. The aircraft was built by a Chinese manufacturer. The demonstration, held in late 2026, marks a significant milestone for autonomous air travel — and a notable win for China's electric aviation industry on Japanese soil. Urban air taxis are expected to be a multi-billion-dollar market by the 2030s, with dozens of companies racing to certify pilotless passenger flight commercially.

Business Q2 / 10

Italian tech firm Bending Spoons recently agreed to buy Mailchimp, the email marketing platform, from Intuit for €3.35 billion. What makes this deal remarkable?

a It is the largest-ever acquisition of a European tech firm by a US company
b Intuit is selling Mailchimp for less than a third of what it paid in 2021
c Bending Spoons is funding the deal entirely through cryptocurrency reserves

b. Intuit is selling Mailchimp at a steep loss, receiving less than a third of the roughly $12 billion it paid when it acquired the platform in 2021. The deal is a rare and expensive admission that the integration simply did not work. Bending Spoons, which also owns WeTransfer and Vimeo, has built a reputation for buying struggling digital products and turning them around.

Business Q3 / 10

More than 30 labour union leaders from 19 countries recently coordinated a call on governments worldwide to review their public contracts with a single company over its worker rights record. Which company?

a Uber
b Meta
c Amazon

c. The campaign targets Amazon and its cloud division, Amazon Web Services. The scale — 30-plus union leaders across 19 countries acting in concert — signals that pressure on Amazon's labour practices has become a genuinely global movement rather than a domestic US story. Governments in several countries hold significant public contracts with AWS for cloud computing infrastructure.

Business Q4 / 10

Anthropic, the AI safety company backed by Google and Amazon, recently made its stock market debut on Nasdaq. How much did it raise in what was reported as the largest IPO in history?

a $23.4 billion
b $81.3 billion
c $54.0 billion

b. Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in its Nasdaq debut, landing a valuation of around $2 trillion. That eclipses every previous IPO on record, including Saudi Aramco's $25.6 billion listing in 2019. The debut was not without drama — broader markets sold off on the day amid investor nerves about whether AI valuations had run too far, too fast.

Economics Q5 / 10

UK inflation (the rate at which prices rise across the economy) hit a 12-month high in October, according to official data released this month. Which of the following best describes the UK's inflation situation right now?

a Prices are falling overall, but the rate of decline is slowing
b Prices are still rising, and the pace of that rise just reached its fastest point in a year
c Inflation has been fully brought under control, with this month's reading a minor blip

b. UK inflation ticked up in October to its highest level in twelve months, meaning prices are still rising and the pace accelerated. This matters because the Bank of England sets interest rates (the cost of borrowing) partly based on inflation data — a stubbornly high reading makes it harder to cut rates and ease pressure on mortgage holders and businesses.

Economics Q6 / 10

President Trump recently signed a law formally ending production of the US one-cent coin — the penny — after more than a century of circulation. Which of the following is NOT a commonly cited reason for scrapping it?

a Each penny costs more than one cent to manufacture
b Most Americans hoard pennies rather than spend them, clogging the money supply
c Electronic payments have made small coins increasingly redundant

b. The 'penny hoarding clogs the money supply' argument is not a standard case made by economists or policymakers. The real reasons are simpler: a penny costs around 3 cents to make, losing taxpayers money on every coin struck, and the rise of card and digital payments has made tiny denominations largely useless. Canada scrapped its penny back in 2013 without noticeable economic disruption.

Economics Q7 / 10

Canada recently expanded tariff relief (reduced import taxes) on pork, seafood, and metals with China, even while still negotiating trade terms with the US. What is driving Canada's pivot toward Beijing?

a China offered Canada a formal free trade agreement, the first with a G7 nation
b Canada is seeking new export markets to reduce its dependence on the US amid American tariff pressure
c A bilateral dispute over Canadian dairy tariffs prompted China to offer a reciprocal deal

b. Canada is diversifying its trade relationships as a hedge against US tariff pressure under Trump. By expanding tariff relief with China on agricultural and industrial goods, Ottawa is signalling it has alternatives to the American market. The US absorbs roughly 75 percent of Canadian exports, making the dependence a genuine vulnerability — and one Canada is now actively trying to reduce.

Economics Q8 / 10

Researchers recently found that consumers in one Southeast Asian country spend an average of $2,063 per person per year on groceries — the highest in the region. Which country?

a Thailand
b Vietnam
c Singapore

c. Singapore tops Southeast Asia for household grocery spending at $2,063 per person annually. This is partly explained by Singapore's very high cost of living — it regularly ranks among the world's most expensive cities — and the fact that it imports over 90 percent of its food, with almost no domestic agriculture, which keeps prices elevated compared to neighbours like Vietnam or Indonesia.

General Knowledge Q9 / 10

Which country is the world's largest producer of coffee, supplying roughly a third of global output?

a Colombia
b Brazil
c Vietnam

b. Brazil is by far the world's largest coffee producer, accounting for around 35 to 40 percent of global supply. It has held the top spot for over 150 years. Vietnam is a distant second, while Colombia — often associated with premium arabica beans — is third. Coffee is one of the most traded commodities on earth, behind only crude oil.

General Knowledge Q10 / 10

Which economist gave us the concept of 'creative destruction' — the idea that capitalism advances by continuously destroying old industries and replacing them with new ones?

a John Maynard Keynes
b Joseph Schumpeter
c Friedrich Hayek

b. Joseph Schumpeter coined 'creative destruction' in his 1942 book Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy. He argued that the engine of capitalism is not price competition but the constant replacement of old technologies and business models by new ones — think steam power giving way to electricity, or Blockbuster giving way to Netflix. It remains one of the most quoted concepts in modern economics.
