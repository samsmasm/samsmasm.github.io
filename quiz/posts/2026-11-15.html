Business Q1 / 10

OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, recently reported quarterly revenue of $10 billion — a 50% jump in just one year. Despite that explosive growth, what did its financial results also show?

a A healthy profit, proving AI can be a real business
b Steep operating losses, meaning it is spending far more than it earns
c Flat costs, suggesting the hard work of scaling is behind it

b. Despite $10 billion in quarterly revenue, OpenAI continues to post steep operating losses. Running the world's most powerful AI models requires enormous computing power, staff, and infrastructure — costs that are growing nearly as fast as income. It is a reminder that high revenue and profitability are very different things, especially in capital-hungry technology businesses.

Business Q2 / 10

German carmaker Mercedes-Benz has recently halted all new investment at its factories in Germany and is considering closing some plants entirely. Which of these best explains why?

a It is relocating all production to the United States to avoid import tariffs
b It is under intense competitive pressure and running major cost-cutting programmes, as are Volkswagen and Audi
c It has decided to pivot entirely to software and exit car manufacturing

b. Mercedes-Benz froze new spending at German sites as it looks for ways to cut costs amid fierce competition, particularly from cheaper Chinese electric vehicles. Volkswagen and Audi have launched similar savings drives. Germany's car industry, long one of its economic crown jewels, is under more pressure than at any point in recent memory.

Business Q3 / 10

ASEAN — the bloc of ten Southeast Asian nations — recently signed a new digital trade agreement. What is the main problem it is trying to solve for businesses selling across the region?

a A shortage of smartphone users in rural areas
b Every country in the bloc has different rules, making cross-border payments and data-sharing complicated and slow
c The region lacks a shared currency, so prices are hard to compare

b. The ASEAN digital trade deal aims to harmonise the patchwork of different national rules around e-commerce, payments, and data. Right now, a business selling to customers across ASEAN has to navigate ten separate regulatory regimes — a costly headache. Southeast Asia's digital economy is projected to exceed $1 trillion by 2030, so smoothing these rules matters enormously.

Business Q4 / 10

A South Korean retailer recently aired what it claims is a home shopping industry first. What made the broadcast genuinely unusual?

a It was filmed entirely underwater, to demonstrate waterproof packaging
b It featured a fully AI-generated host selling Jeju kiwis on a show made entirely by artificial intelligence
c It was presented live by the CEO's golden retriever, trained over six months

b. Hyundai Home Shopping debuted a regularly scheduled sales programme made entirely with artificial intelligence, complete with an AI host flogging Jeju Heart Gold kiwis. It is believed to be the first of its kind in the home shopping industry. The choice of product — a premium Korean fruit — was deliberately relatable, perhaps to ease viewers into the slightly uncanny experience of buying groceries from a non-human presenter.

Economics Q5 / 10

The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the third quarter — GDP (gross domestic product, the total value of everything a country produces) came in exactly as economists expected. Which sector was a surprisingly strong driver of that growth?

a North Sea oil and gas extraction, boosted by high global energy prices
b Computer programming and consultancy, lifted by rising demand for AI and cloud computing
c Construction, following a surge in new housing starts across England

b. Computer programming and consultancy services were among the standout contributors to UK GDP growth in Q3, as businesses rushed to adopt AI and cloud computing tools. It is a striking sign of how the AI investment boom — often discussed in terms of US tech giants — is showing up in British economic data. Services now account for roughly 80% of UK output, so what happens in tech matters a great deal.

Economics Q6 / 10

The US Federal Reserve (America's central bank, which sets interest rates) recently announced it will skip its October 2027 policy vote entirely — holding a strategy meeting instead. This is the first step in a broader overhaul under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Which of these is NOT a risk associated with changing how the Fed operates?

a Markets becoming uncertain about when and how interest rate decisions will be made
b A reduction in the Fed's credibility if the changes look politically motivated
c Inflation automatically rising because fewer votes are held each year

c. Inflation does not rise automatically because a central bank holds fewer scheduled votes — that is not how monetary policy works. The genuine risks are reputational and market-related: investors rely on a predictable Fed calendar, and any hint that changes are politically driven could undermine the central bank's hard-won independence. The Fed's credibility is one of its most valuable — and fragile — assets.

Economics Q7 / 10

China and Türkiye recently signed a $15 billion deal focused on renewable energy. What will the money be used for, and why does Türkiye want it?

a Building nuclear power plants, as Türkiye phases out coal by 2030
b Expanding solar and wind capacity, as Türkiye seeks foreign investment to hit its clean energy targets by 2040
c Purchasing Chinese electric vehicles for a new state-run transport network

b. The $15 billion deal will channel Chinese investment into Turkish solar and wind projects, helping Türkiye close a large gap between its current energy mix and its 2040 clean energy ambitions. For China, it is another opportunity to export its world-leading solar manufacturing capacity. Türkiye sits at the crossroads of Europe and Asia, making it a strategically valuable partner for Beijing's green energy diplomacy.

Economics Q8 / 10

US energy costs are rising this winter, but the increase is very uneven. Average household heating costs are up around 7% — but one specific fuel is set to cost dramatically more. How much are heating oil prices expected to rise?

a Around 15%, roughly double the average
b Around 35%, reflecting tighter global supply
c Around 70%, due to sharply reduced supply caused by the war in Iran

c. Heating oil prices are expected to surge around 70% this winter — far more than the 7% average headline figure — because the war in Iran has severely disrupted supply. Heating oil is used mainly in the northeastern United States, where older homes rely on it rather than gas. This is a sharp illustration of how geopolitical shocks translate directly into household bills for specific communities.

General Knowledge Q9 / 10

Which country is the world's largest producer of lithium — the metal that is essential for electric vehicle batteries and energy storage?

a Chile
b Australia
c China

b. Australia is the world's largest lithium producer, accounting for roughly half of global mine output. Chile holds the largest known reserves, stored in its famous salt flat brines, and ranks second in production. China dominates the processing and refining of lithium even though it mines less of it — giving it enormous influence over the battery supply chain regardless.

General Knowledge Q10 / 10

Which economist gave us the idea of 'creative destruction' — the theory that capitalism advances by constantly tearing down old industries and replacing them with new ones?

a John Maynard Keynes
b Joseph Schumpeter
c Milton Friedman

b. Joseph Schumpeter, an Austrian economist writing in the mid-20th century, coined the term 'creative destruction' to describe how innovation kills off old businesses and industries while generating new ones. He saw it as capitalism's defining feature — not a bug, but the engine. The term is now used constantly to describe everything from streaming services destroying video rental shops to AI reshaping white-collar work.
