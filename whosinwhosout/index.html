How it works

  1. Average the latest poll from each pollster in the last days, each rescaled to 100%, weighted by recency (half-life days) and by the square root of sample size.
  2. Give out 120 seats by Sainte-Laguë among parties with 5% of the party vote or at least one electorate.
  3. Decide each of the 71 electorates: by default apply each party's national swing to the 2023 candidate vote (a few seats are called from electorate polls); or let the party that won the closest 2023 seat hold it.
  4. A party that wins more electorates than its share keeps them all (overhang), so the House can be bigger than 120.
  5. Fill each party's seats with its electorate winners first, then down its list, skipping anyone already in.
