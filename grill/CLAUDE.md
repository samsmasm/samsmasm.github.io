# Grill (graph drill), `/grill`

Drills IB Economics government intervention diagrams: indirect tax, subsidy, price ceiling, price floor (2.7 in the 2027 guide: diagrams SL/HL, calculations HL only), since 2026-11-12 the four externalities with their interventions (2.8), and since 2026-11-16 minimum wage and PED and total revenue. Built 2026-10-29 as a quick prototype for a class test a few days later; later plans are more micro graphs, then macro.

## Externalities (added 2026-11-12, Sam's spec)
- Home grid has two sections. Externality rows: negative/positive production/consumption (`negprod` `posprod` `negcons` `poscons`), plus an amber **All four mixed** row (pseudo-kind `ext`): it deals types from a shuffled deck (`session.deck`: every four questions cover all four, never the same type twice running; plain random gave Sam long runs of one type) and its cells show the four rows' scores added together. Scores are always recorded under the real type.
- Any question about the price must have that price labelled (Sam): quota graphs label Pc where the quota meets demand; a test checks every price question has Pc or P₂ on the graph.
- Each question picks an intervention (`sc.iv`) at random from `EXT_IVS[kind]` (`none` twice, so the plain market failure comes up most): neg prod: tax, partial tax, quota, regulation; pos prod: subsidy, partial subsidy; neg cons: tax, partial tax, quota, regulation, education; pos cons: subsidy, partial subsidy, direct provision, advertising. Sam ticked all of these.
- Labels follow IBecon's graphs.md: production `MSB = D`, `MPC = S`, `MSC`; consumption `MPB = D`, `MSC = S`, `MSB`; points Pm/Qm (market), P*/Q* (optimum). Social curves are red. A full tax/subsidy on production lies exactly on MSC, so it is one line named MSC. Partial: `S + tax` with Qt, `S + subsidy` with Q2 (as the tax/subsidy drills). Regulation = rules that cut the harm per unit: MSC₁ → MSC₂ (or MSB₁ → MSB₂), Q*₁ → Q*₂, market stays at Qm (Sam's "regulations that can reduce the externality"). Quota = vertical line at Q*. Direct provision = `S₂` through D at Q*, price P₂. Education/advertising = MPB₁ → MPB₂ part of the way to MSB, new market P₂/Q₂ (as IBecon 2.8). Tradable permits, bans and compulsory-consumption laws are Effects questions only (no graph).
- Draw steps must never show the answer line already drawn (Sam spotted MSC labelled on the full-tax draw step, where the taxed supply lands on MSC). `diagramExt` leaves MSC off `base` for full production tax/subsidy; a test checks the right line is never in `drawBase`.
- Externality shading: every area of the chart is tappable (Sam, 2026-11-12), not just areas under demand as in the price-control drills, so areas above D don't hint at the answer (e.g. the external benefit band above MPB).
- `genExt` keeps every labelled value and every corner of every shading area on a gridline inside the chart, and rejects markets whose label boxes touch (`labelsFit`, including the "Price, cost, benefit ($)" axis title box). HL calcs that match a shading area carry `concept`; a test checks the answer equals the area.
- Code: `diagramExt`, `conceptsExt`, `effectsExt`, `calcsExt`, `lineCandsExt`, `labelWhyExt`, `contextExt`; the shared functions dispatch to these with `isExt(sc)`.
- "Totally random", "Practise my weakest" and the skill column buttons cover every topic, so weakest sends students to untried boxes first. Section headings drill only their own section (Step 3).

## How it works
- **Markets are generated**, never hand-written: straight-line D (`y = a − b·x`) and S (`y = c + d·x`) on a 12 × 14 grid (x = quantity / 10, y = price in $), slopes 0.5/1/2, every key point a whole number (`gen(kind)`). Calculation answers come from the same numbers.
- `diagram(sc)`: the finished diagram (lines, points with P/Q axis names, Shortage/Surplus braces). Labels, slots and the drawn picture all come from it.
- **Skills** (columns on the home grid): Draw (tap the right grey line; ceilings/floors may follow up with "raise/lower it" + an effects question, then a label step), Label (tap chip, tap box), Shade, Effects (multiple choice), Calculate (HL; "Read graph" at SL, see below).
- **Shading**: `conceptsFor(sc)` lists each area as a polygon (CS/PS before and after, DWL, tax revenue, subsidy cost, expenditure, revenue, tax burden, transfers, government buying the surplus). `buildCells` cuts the chart along every line plus horizontals/verticals at the key points, and groups cells by which concepts contain them, so any concept is a whole number of tappable areas. To add an area, just add a concept polygon whose edges lie on those lines.
- **Labels** match the IBecon diagrams: Pe, Qe, Pc, Pp, Qt (tax), Q2 (subsidy; Sam prefers Qe then Q2 over Q1), S + tax, S + subsidy, plus Pmax/Pmin, Qs/Qd, Price ceiling/floor.
- **Wrong answers** (Sam, 2026-10-29): say "Not quite. Try again." and leave the step editable at once, mistakes in red (wrong line, red label boxes, red shaded areas, greyed MC option). Only the first attempt is scored; a later right answer says "Got it". "Show me the answer" (`finish(false)`) reveals. `check()` marks, `finish(ok)` closes the step.
- **Don't give answers away on the graph**: multiple-choice steps draw the diagram without the Shortage/Surplus labels (`noAnno`). Sam spotted "What does the floor create?" with "Surplus" written on the graph.
- **What-if questions** ("the ceiling is set above Pe...") contradict the drawn market, so they carry `noGraph`: no chart, no context line.
- **Label placement** (`labelSpots`): line labels sit at the line's end (top edge centred, right edge to the right, bottom edge just above). Any box that would touch another slides along its line in 24px steps (falling lines down, rising lines and top-edge labels up); horizontal lines never move. Students must always see one box per label: a student (2026-11-12) found D's box hidden under the Price ceiling box when D left the chart at the ceiling price. A test checks no two boxes overlap across 1,200+ generated markets; externality generators also reject any market where `labelsFit` fails. `slotWidth`: 40px, 52px for 5 to 6 characters, 92px for longer names.
- **Labels**: drag a chip (or a filled box) onto a box, or tap chip then box (`placeLabel`, pointer-event drag with a ghost chip). A wrong check sends the wrong labels back to the list and leaves their boxes empty and red.
- **Skip question** button: new question, nothing scored.
- **Choosing a drill**: Totally random (`mix`), Practise my weakest (`weak`), a section heading (`sect`: that section's topics, random skill), a topic row name (`kind`: random skill), a skill column name (`skill`: random topic), or one box (`cell`).
- **SL does no calculations** (Sam, 2026-10-29, reversing an earlier "SL number questions" request). At SL the Calculate column is called "Read graph" (`skillName`) and asks "What is Pc?" etc. for every labelled point (`readsFor`, 3 per question). HL gets `calcsFor` (spending, revenue, government money, shortage/surplus, CS/PS, DWL, shares, transfers). Same `calc` score key at both levels. **No Qd/Qs functions anywhere**: the 2027 guide doesn't use them and Sam doesn't want students thinking they need them. Every value is read off the graph, so generators keep all needed points on gridlines (including the DWL corner for ceilings/floors).
- **Shading** puts DWL in about half of shade questions (Sam wants DWL identification drilled).
- **Report a problem**: link under each question opens an inline panel (reason chips + optional text) and POSTs to the shared feedback Worker with `app: 'grill'`; `diagram` holds the question, market and the student's answer as JSON (shown under "Attached data" in the inbox).
- **Enter key**: marks the answer, then a second Enter moves on. The keydown handler calls `preventDefault()`, otherwise focus moves to Next during marking and the same Enter press clicks it (Sam hit this).
- **Progress**: localStorage `grill_v1` = `{ level, scores: { 'tax.shade': [last 10 as 1/0] } }`. "Practise my weakest" picks untried cells first, then the lowest score.
- Floor welfare (CS/PS/DWL) assumes the government does NOT buy the surplus; the "government buys the surplus" questions say so.
