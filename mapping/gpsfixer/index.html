2Choose what to fix

Long jumps between recorded points are listed below. They are only suspects: some straight sections are real.

Works for stray points too, where the GPS wandered off while still recording: pick the last good point before them and the first good one after. Everything in between is replaced.

3Draw the real route

  • Click the map to add points, in order, from A towards B.
  • Drag an orange point to move it. Click one to remove it.
  • Drag a hollow midpoint to bend a line.
  • Click a line to switch it between straight (dashed) and following roads (solid).

New lines follow the map between the points you click, so a few clicks cover every turn. Uses the free OSRM router run by FOSSGIS, which receives the points you click (never your file).

–

4Calculate timestamps

Spreads the time between A and B along your route by distance, so the section is covered at a constant average speed. These are estimates, not recovered GPS data.

5Review and download

Fix as many gaps as you like before downloading. Click a repair to go back to it. Undo and redo (top right of the map, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) work across all of them.

Upload the downloaded file to Strava as a new activity (delete the old one first if you want to replace it). Strava works out its own distance from the track, so it may differ a little from the figure here.

Privacy: your GPX file is read, edited and saved entirely in this browser. Nothing is uploaded. Map tiles (OpenStreetMap data, served by CARTO) reveal roughly which area you are looking at, as any web map does. If you turn on "Follow roads", the points you click are sent to the FOSSGIS routing server to find the roads between them.
