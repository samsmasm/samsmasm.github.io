Engine and suspension

Gearing is top wheel speed. Light bodies accelerate harder but grip less. Soft suspension (low Hz) soaks up bumps.

Body corners

Bigger body, more engine torque, but more weight. Corners re-sort by angle when you let go.

Wheels

A wheel sits where its angle meets the body's edge, so it stays put when you change corners. 0° is straight ahead, 270° straight down.

How the cars work

Engine torque comes from body area, shared between the wheels. A big body is strong but heavy.

Big wheels give more clearance and speed for the same gearing; small wheels push harder.

On the sprint, a light car with high gearing tends to win. Hills want torque and grip. Rough ground rewards soft suspension and a long, low wheelbase.
