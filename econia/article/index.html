Stage 2

Pick your article

The most important decision you will make. Get this right and the rest flows.

A good article makes the commentary almost write itself. A bad one makes every sentence a struggle. Spend real time here — it is worth it.

Three things that matter for any article

1. You need two diagrams — so you need two things to show.
Your commentary requires two diagrams. That means the article must contain two separate economic moments worth analysing, not just one. The clearest structure is an initial event and a response: a shock and then a policy reaction, a market failure and then an intervention. If the article only describes one thing happening with no follow-through, you will not be able to draw two distinct diagrams. Check this before you commit.

2. Simple is better.
Resist the temptation to choose an unusual or complex article because it seems more interesting. A clean, familiar topic with a clear intervention gives you everything you need. Complicated or multi-layered articles tend to cause problems at every subsequent stage.

3. You can only analyse what the article actually says — so it must contain enough.
You cannot use economic knowledge that seems obvious but is not stated in the article. If the article does not explain why something is happening, you cannot invent a cause. If it does not mention a trade-off, you cannot add one. The article is the boundary of your analysis. This means the article itself must contain enough specific detail — causes, figures, policy responses, stakeholder reactions — to give you material to work with. A short article that vaguely describes a problem without explaining it will leave you with nothing to analyse.

The admin checklist

Run every article you are seriously considering through this list. The unit-specific content checks are on each unit page above.

The Must-Haves
Is it recent? Published within 12 months of when you write the commentary. Six months is even better.
Is it from a unique source? You must use a different news outlet for each of your three commentaries.
Is it published news media? Newspapers, journals, reputable news sites. Not blogs, opinion pieces, social media posts, or government reports.
Is it the right syllabus unit? Each commentary must cover a different unit: Microeconomics (Unit 2), Macroeconomics (Unit 3), or The Global Economy (Unit 4).
Is it an actual news item? Avoid opinion pieces, blog entries, lifestyle content, advertisements, or government reports.
Strategic Source Selection
Is it neutral reportage? Sources like BBC, CNN, Al Jazeera, or The Guardian report facts and stakeholder reactions without doing the economic analysis for you.
Does it avoid expert analysis? (The only item where you want a "no") Publications like The Economist or the FT often provide the economic analysis themselves, leaving you little room to show your own insight.
Is it a manageable length? One to two pages is ideal. Shorter and focused is better than long and wandering.
The Two-Diagram Test
Can you identify two separate moments to diagram? The first is usually the event or problem. The second is the response or consequence. Both must be present in the article. See your unit page for what this looks like in practice.
