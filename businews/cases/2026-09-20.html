Seized Without Warning: When Russia Takes Control of Your Business

Imagine building a business in a foreign country for decades, only to have the government take control of it overnight. That is exactly what happened to Nestlé, Auchan, and other Western companies operating in Russia in 2026.

Source: euronews.com →

Background

Nestlé is a Swiss multinational food and beverage company, one of the largest in the world, producing brands ranging from KitKat to Nescafé. Auchan is a French retail giant operating large supermarkets across Europe and Asia. Both companies maintained significant operations in Russia long after many Western rivals exited following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

What Happened

  • In September 2026, the Russian government placed the local subsidiaries of Nestlé, Auchan, and several other Western-linked companies under 'temporary administration', meaning Russian state-appointed managers took over day-to-day control.
  • Nestlé confirmed it was assessing its options, signalling uncertainty about whether it could recover any control over or value from its Russian assets.
  • Auchan and Lemana Pro (a home improvement retailer) stated publicly that their Russian operations were continuing as normal, despite the administrative takeover.
  • The move was widely seen as retaliation by Russia against Western economic sanctions and the broader geopolitical conflict stemming from the war in Ukraine.

The Tension

For companies like Nestlé and Auchan, Russia represented years of capital investment and brand-building. Exiting early, as some rivals chose to do in 2022, would have meant writing off those assets entirely. Staying, however, exposed them to exactly this risk: a host government using political leverage to strip foreign owners of operational control. The core tension is between short-term asset protection and long-term reputational damage. Staying in Russia after 2022 attracted fierce criticism from consumers and shareholders in Western markets, threatening brand equity built over generations. Yet leaving meant accepting certain financial losses, whereas staying preserved at least the possibility of future recovery. Now, with state administration imposed, companies face a third, grimmer scenario: losses without the choice. The episode illustrates how the external political environment can override even the most carefully constructed market strategy.

Outside View

Many analysts argued that companies which stayed in Russia after 2022 were taking on political risk that their financial models could not adequately price. Critics noted that continuing to generate tax revenue for the Russian state, even unintentionally, made Western multinationals uncomfortable participants in the broader geopolitical conflict. Some investors began applying greater scrutiny to the geopolitical exposure of multinationals, treating a large Russian footprint as a governance red flag rather than simply a market opportunity.
