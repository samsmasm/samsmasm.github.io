America's Enterprising Spirit Is Booming After Decades-Long Slump

nytimes.com

Hundreds of thousands of people are creating companies each year, ending a decades-long drought.

  • What do you think changed to suddenly make hundreds of thousands of people confident enough to start their own businesses after decades of decline? Could the same conditions exist in Vietnam or other Asian countries right now?
  • When lots of new small businesses are created, some will fail and some will succeed. Who should bear the cost of those failures, and why? The entrepreneurs themselves, the banks that lend to them, or society through safety nets?

War Risk for Businesses Will Mean Higher Prices No Matter What Happens

nytimes.com

Doing business in a more dangerous and unpredictable world will cost more, pushing up the price of everything from food to electronics.

  • If prices rise due to war and geopolitical risk, who gets hurt the most: wealthy people who can absorb the costs, or poor people who spend most of their income on necessities? What responsibility does this create for governments or businesses?
  • Companies might choose to move factories away from risky regions or hold larger safety stock, both of which cost money. Should consumers pay higher prices to support this extra safety, or should companies absorb the costs themselves?

Chinese automakers are taking on the UK, and many Brits are embracing it

cnbc.com

The surge comes as China's auto exports have boomed in recent years as the country's appetite for new models has cooled.

  • If Chinese cars are cheaper or better quality, why should UK consumers prefer British brands? Is there a good economic reason to 'buy local', or is that just patriotism that hurts your wallet?
  • China's car industry is booming partly because demand at home has slowed down. What does this tell us about how businesses find new markets when growth stops in their home country? Could Vietnamese companies do the same thing someday?

Home sales in Europe: Where did they rise most in 2025?

euronews.com

Home sales rose in 17 of 20 European countries in 2025 as lower borrowing costs helped revive demand. France recorded more than one million transactions, while Slovenia saw the sharpest percentage increase.

  • When interest rates fall and more people can afford to buy homes, is that purely good news, or could it create problems? Think about what happens to house prices when demand suddenly increases.
  • Home buying boomed when borrowing became cheaper. But in your own country, are there young people who still can't afford homes even with lower interest rates? What other barriers might exist beyond just the cost of borrowing?

Casio finds new cash cow in once maligned 'cheap' watches

asia.nikkei.com

Casio Computer's share price is picking up as the company finds new profitability in affordable watch segments once dismissed by competitors.

  • Why would competitors have ignored cheap watches as 'unprofitable' when Casio is now making serious money from them? What does this tell us about assumptions that successful businesses make, and when those assumptions can be wrong?
  • Casio's luxury watch competitors probably looked down on the cheap watch market. Has this attitude of dismissing budget products changed in other industries, or do rich brands still ignore low-end markets? What are the risks of doing so?

IMF expects world economy to grow a sluggish 3% this year, weighed down by Iran war but helped by AI

apnews.com

The International Monetary Fund projects global economic growth of 3% this year, with geopolitical tensions offsetting gains from artificial intelligence developments.

  • The IMF sees AI as a major boost to the economy, but also sees war reducing growth. If AI makes some workers more productive, what happens to workers whose jobs AI can do? Does the economy-wide benefit get shared fairly, or do some people bear the costs while others get the gains?
  • Is 3% economic growth good or bad? For who? What would need to happen for you to think growth is too slow, and what would be the consequences of accepting slower growth?
