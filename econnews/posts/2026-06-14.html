Drugmakers are betting they can crack into the obesity drug market that Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly currently dominate.

  • If these obesity drugs are expensive and only available to wealthy people, does that create an unfair health advantage based on income? How is that different from or similar to other inequalities you see in society?

Ebola: Private clinics on front line as patients shun public hospitals

africanews.com

In Bunia, the epicenter of the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, the city's private clinics are playing a major role in receiving and referring patients, as the population is reluctant to go to public hospitals for fear of contracting the virus

  • When people don't trust public hospitals during a disease outbreak, how does that make controlling the disease harder? What would need to happen to rebuild that trust?
  • Should governments rely on private clinics during health emergencies, or does this create problems for how health care is organized overall?

UK economy shrinks in April as the Iran war energy shock takes a toll

euronews.com

Britain's economy contracted by 0.1% in April, the first monthly fall since August 2025, as the conflict in the Middle East pushed up energy costs and prompted households to rein in their spending.

  • How does a war halfway around the world end up affecting whether families in the UK can afford heating or electricity? Can countries really insulate themselves from global shocks?
  • When energy prices rise, who gets hurt the most in society, and who gets hurt the least? Should governments do anything special to protect certain groups?

Zimbabwe's traditional food revival gains momentum

euronews.com

From bubbling sorghum porridge to village-reared chicken and ox-head stew, traditional food restaurants are booming in Zimbabwe's second city, Bulawayo. Driven by health-conscious consumers and nostalgia for homegrown flavours, indigenous cuisine is making a strong comeback.

  • What does it say about a society when people suddenly get excited about food their grandparents ate after preferring imported or modern foods? Is this progress, nostalgia, or something else?
  • Could this trend help Zimbabwe's rural farmers and small producers make more money, or does it mainly benefit restaurant owners in the city?
