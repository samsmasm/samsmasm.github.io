Europe's scorching summer could cost EU economies €180 billion

euronews.com

Extreme heat could cost EU economies around €180 billion this year, roughly 1% of GDP and almost exactly the growth the bloc had been expecting for 2026, with France facing the heaviest blow of the big member states.

  • The article shows extreme weather wiping out an entire year's economic growth for Europe. If climate-related costs keep growing like this, how should countries decide where to invest: preventing climate change or adapting to its impacts?
  • Vietnam faces similar climate risks from typhoons and flooding. Should the government spend money on climate prevention now, or wait until the damage is bigger and more obvious to taxpayers?

Humanoids, human-shaped robots, at this BMW factory in South Carolina are expected to respond to voice commands, solve problems and react to unforeseen events. U.S. automakers hope that this technology can give them a fighting chance against their Chinese rivals that enjoy lower costs.

  • If robots become better and cheaper than human workers, should governments tax companies that use robots, or is that unfair to businesses trying to stay competitive?
  • Imagine you're deciding between a career in manufacturing or car design. How would the rise of humanoid robots change what kind of job you'd want ten years from now?

  • Shipping companies have to choose between a faster route through Suez that might be risky, or a longer, safer route around Africa that costs more. What information would you need to make that decision, and would you ever choose the riskier path?

IEA and OPEC split on global oil demand estimates as Strait of Hormuz closure drags

euronews.com

The world's two most watched oil forecasters published sharply conflicting views of 2026 on Wednesday, with the IEA deepening its call for the first annual demand decline since the pandemic while OPEC still sees consumption growing, as the closure of the Strait of Hormuz drags on.

  • Two major organizations disagree completely on whether oil demand will rise or fall. Why might they have different forecasts, and does this disagreement make their predictions less trustworthy?
  • If oil demand actually declines as the IEA predicts, what happens to oil-producing countries that depend on oil sales, and should richer nations help them transition to other industries?

Azerbaijan bets on the pomegranate as its next big export

euronews.com

Azerbaijan is turning pomegranates, a fruit with 2,000 years of cultural history, into a global brand, with €48.2 million in exports and more than 200 cultivated varieties putting it in direct competition with Iran, Turkey and Spain.

  • Azerbaijan is competing against much larger countries in pomegranate exports. What does a small country need to do to win against bigger rivals, and is this strategy better or worse than trying to compete on price?
  • Many Southeast Asian countries have similar opportunities to build global brands around local crops or products. What would it take to make Vietnamese agricultural products as recognizable worldwide as French wine or Italian pasta?
