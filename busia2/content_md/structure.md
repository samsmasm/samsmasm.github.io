# Structure

The IB specifies three sections for your 1,800 words, and you can see them in action in the [Sample IA](../sample/).

> **One word matters: appropriate.** Criterion F rewards structure that fits your research question, not a generic template.

## Section 1: Introduction (~400 words)

- **The Company (Background):** its industry, market position and core activities.
- **The Issue (The Problem):** the decision or issue you are investigating, and why it matters.
- **The Concept (The Lens):** your key concept and why it is relevant to your RQ.
- **The Methodology (Research Approach):** your sources and why you chose them, and the tools you will apply.

## Section 2: Analysis and Discussion (~1,100 words)

This is where structure matters most. Choose an organising principle that comes from your RQ:

- **For vs. against:** useful for should/shouldn't questions
- **Internal vs. external stakeholder impacts:** how different groups are affected
- **Ethical / evaluative framing:** "yes, the practice is justified because..." then "no, it is not because..."
- **By business function:** finance, operations, marketing, HR
- **By analytical tool:** one tool per sub-section, each covering a *different aspect* of the RQ

The test: could someone follow your argument from your headings alone? Apply each tool to your organisation and documents, not as a textbook exercise.

**Evaluate the limits of your evidence as you go.** End each sub-section with a sentence or two on how far the evidence can be trusted (for example, a source with an interest in the outcome, one year of figures, or a survey of one group). If your SDs disagree, say which is more convincing and why. This is where Criterion D (5 marks) rewards you.

## Section 3: Conclusion (~300 words)

- **Answer the research question directly** with a clear judgment.
- **Summarise the main evidence** that led to it, without new analysis or new information.
- **Return to the key concept.** What does this case show about how it works in practice?
- **Qualify your answer only with points already made** in your analysis. Limitations of your evidence belong in the analysis (Criterion D).

## What does not count toward the word limit

These do not count: the supporting documents, diagrams and figures, tables of statistical data, equations and calculations, in-text citations, references, the bibliography, the contents page, and acknowledgements. Everything else in the main commentary counts, including definitions and quotes.

## Choosing your tools

The IB sets no number of tools and Kognity recommends two to four, but aim for **two to three**, because more usually means too little depth for Criterion C. Apply every tool in the main body, not the appendix. Any tool from the course can be used: see the Theories and tools page in Kognity's IA section.

**Strong choices:**

- 7Ps Marketing Mix
- Triple Bottom Line
- Investment Appraisal (NPV, payback)
- Profitability Ratios
- Ansoff Matrix
- Porter's Five Forces
- Lewin's Change Model
- Maslow's Hierarchy
- Boston Matrix

**Use with caution:**

- SWOT (too descriptive as a primary tool)
- STEEPLE (same issue - tends to become a list)
- Force Field Analysis (often superficial)
- Generic "stakeholder analysis" without depth

SWOT and STEEPLE are not banned, but as your main framework they invite description. Build your analytical core around a tool that forces you to take a position.

You do not need the full framework of any tool, but name the parts you use. For example: "The environmental and technological dimensions of a STEEPLE analysis are most relevant to this question."

Financial ratios (profitability, liquidity) are essential if your RQ touches on financial performance or market share. HL students: an HL tool is not required, but it tends to make an IA stand out.

> **Go deep, not wide.** One tool applied with real thinking impresses more than a shallow run through five frameworks.
