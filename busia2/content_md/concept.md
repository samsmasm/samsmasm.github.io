# Key Concept

Criterion A (Key Concept) is worth 5 of your 25 marks, the joint highest criterion. It rewards work where the concept shapes the research and runs through the analysis and conclusion, not just the introduction. Choose one of the four concepts: not two, and not a blend.

> **Label vs. engine.** At a 2, the concept is a label attached to the work. At a 5, it is the engine: if you deleted every mention of it, the argument would fall apart.

## The four concepts

### Change

A shift in a business's strategy, structure, market, product or environment. Ask why it changed, how the change was managed, and whether it worked.

### Creativity

New ideas, products, processes or business models. It is broader than invention: it includes creative strategy and new ways of organising.

### Ethics

The principles behind business decisions, such as environmental impact, labour practices or data privacy. A strong ethics IA analyses the trade-off between profit and responsibility rather than just judging.

### Sustainability

Meeting present needs without compromising the future, whether environmental, social or economic. Use it for long-term viability or for balancing profit, planet and people.

## How to choose your concept

Do not pick a company first and attach a concept afterwards. Start with a conceptual question, such as *"Is this company's practice ethical?"* or *"Is this strategy sustainable?"*, and let it become your RQ. Then check:

- Can I use this concept to frame my analysis tools?
- Can I say something meaningful about it in my conclusion?
- Does it help me evaluate, not just describe?

If the answer to any of these is no, try another concept.

> **Put the concept word in your RQ.** It is not required, but strong IAs do it, and it keeps you returning to the concept.

## How to use the concept throughout the IA

Use it in at least four places, adding something each time.

### 1. Introduction: define it

Explain what the concept means for your business, rather than quoting the IB glossary.

### 2. Analysis: apply it

Use the concept as a lens on what your tools show. If your concept is sustainability, your Triple Bottom Line analysis should examine how the business balances people, planet and profit.

**Choose tools that connect to the concept.** For ethics, a stakeholder analysis will generate ethical findings, while a generic SWOT probably will not.

**End each section by connecting back to the concept.** After analysing revenue trends, add a sentence on what they mean for sustainability.

### 3. Evaluation: test it

Has the business genuinely embodied the concept, or only superficially? Where does it create trade-offs?

### 4. Conclusion: reflect on it

Say something non-obvious about what this case shows about the concept in practice.

> **Put it on your title page.** State your key concept there, alongside your RQ and word count.

> **Practical test.** Search your draft for the concept word and its synonyms. Sections without it are pulling your A mark down.
