# Supporting Documents

Your supporting documents are the evidence base for your research, and you should aim for five. They sit in your appendix, and they are the raw material you analyse, not just references. Almost all the evidence in your commentary should trace back to them.

## The rules

- **3 to 5 documents - aim for 5.** Only go lower if your documents are genuinely stronger without the extras. One or two, or more than five, caps you at 1 mark for Criterion B, regardless of quality.
- **Published within the last three years** of your submission date. For older figures, use a recent annual report that includes earlier years.
- **Primary and/or secondary.** All-secondary is fine, and a strong base of secondary sources works best.
- **Balanced.** They should differ in the type of information, the source and the perspective (see Check 1 below).
- **Directly relevant to your RQ.** If a document does not help you answer it, leave it out.
- **Your argument stands on them.** Other sources can add context, but they must never carry the argument.

## Primary vs. secondary sources

**Primary: you generate these yourself.** If it already existed, it is secondary.

- Interview with a manager, employee, or customer
- Survey you designed
- Focus group
- Field observation notes
- Personal communication from the business

**Secondary: published by someone else.**

- News article from a reputable outlet
- The company's own documents: reports, website, social media
- Industry market research
- Academic journal article
- Government or NGO data
- Transcript of a video or audio file (max one per IA)

## What makes a strong supporting document?

- It contains specific data: numbers, quotes, decisions, timelines.
- It comes from a credible, identifiable source.
- **It is five pages or fewer.** Trim longer documents to the relevant sections.
- **The parts you use are highlighted**, so the examiner can see where your evidence comes from.

## Common mistakes

**Using Wikipedia or ready-made analyses.** Wikipedia, textbooks, and SWOT or PESTLE analyses downloaded from the internet are not suitable. Use the original source that Wikipedia cites.

**Collecting documents before you know your RQ.** Know your RQ first, then collect evidence to answer it.

**Forgetting to label them.** Label each one (Supporting document 1, Supporting document 2...) and use that label in the commentary.

## Document Treasure Hunt

Build a provisional list with more than enough to choose from later. Find at least one document for each target, and note its source, date and what it contains.

*Your provisional research question:* ___________________________________________

**Target 1: A news article**
Something that has happened to or around your business recently.

**Target 2: Company-produced document (not financial statements)**
An annual report narrative, sustainability report, investor presentation or press release.

**Target 3: Financial statements**
An income statement, balance sheet or cash flow statement. For private companies, look for figures in databases or the news.

**Target 4: Numbers someone else has given about this company**
An analyst report, market research, a consumer survey or a ranking.

**Target 5: Industry or market-level source**
Market research, trade association data or competitor analysis.

**Target 6: Two documents that show things in a different light**
Sources that raise concerns or sit in tension with your other evidence.

**Target 7: A source from outside your country**
This tests whether your RQ is local or part of a wider pattern.

**Target 8: Other documents that do not fit the above**
Government regulations, NGO reports, academic studies, court documents, staff reviews or transcripts can give your IA an angle others will not have.

## Locking in your final five

Do two checks before you commit.

**Check 1: Balance.** Do your five documents vary in:

- the **types of information** they provide (financial data, stakeholder views, market data, company narrative)?
- the **sources** they come from (not all the company's own PR)?
- the **perspectives** they offer (some support, some challenge)?

**Check 2: The highlight test.** Highlight everything you will quote or reference, and copy it into your IA plan. If you have highlighted only five or eight sentences from a document, it is a crutch, not a real supporting document.

> **The documents are your evidence, not your IA.** Use them: reference specific data, quote key statements, and build your analysis from what they show.
