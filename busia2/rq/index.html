Find Your Research Question

This is where most students get stuck. Too broad and you cannot go deep. Too narrow and you cannot find evidence. Too vague and you cannot reach a conclusion.

What makes a good RQ?

A good BM IA research question:

  • Names a specific business
  • Focuses on a real decision, change, problem, or challenge it has faced
  • Connects to one of the four key concepts, ideally with the concept word in the question
  • Is genuinely open - you do not already know the answer
  • Can be investigated using 3-5 real documents
  • Uses a different business and topic from your BM extended essay, if you are writing one

Backward-looking RQs (a recent issue) are safer than forward-looking ones (an issue the business faces now), which still need backward-looking data from your SDs. Draft two or three and compare them.

Some examples

ChangeBackward-looking

"How successful was Starbucks's 2024 'Back to Starbucks' change in strategy in restoring sales growth?"

SustainabilityBackward-looking

"Did Unilever's 2024 decision to scale back its sustainability targets harm its brand image?"

CreativityForward-looking

"How can airline X successfully target segment Y?"

EthicsChangeForward-looking

"Should Nintendo change its focus from making Rated M games to exclusively kid-friendly content to maintain its brand image?"

What kind of business should you choose?

Pick one with plenty of secondary sources: accounts, annual reports, reliable news. A large international public company is usually strongest.

Private Public
Local There is very little secondary data. Only choose one after checking it carefully with me. This can work, if there are enough independent documents.
International There are no published accounts, so this only works for RQs that do not need financial data, such as marketing or brand ethics. This is the strongest choice, with plenty of reports, accounts and news.

Activity 2

News Hunt: finding a researchable business

1Pick a sector. Retail, tech, food, sport, fashion, healthcare - anything.

2Search recent news. Try Google News or BBC Business: "[sector] company decision 2026" or "[sector] ethics controversy". Look for a named business that has actually done something.

3Check documents exist. Is there an annual or sustainability report, and two or three articles on this decision? If so, pursue it.

4Draft a rough RQ. "Was [business]'s [decision] successful in [achieving what]?" (backward-looking) or "Should [business] [do something specific] to [achieve what]?" (forward-looking). Closed questions are easier to conclude than "To what extent...". It does not need to be perfect yet.

Activity 3

Two routes to a topic

1Start with a concept. Find it in the business world: a change of direction, a greenwashing accusation, an ethics controversy, a creative business model. That becomes your company and your RQ.

2Start with a company. Look for links to the concepts:

  • Changing strategy, structure, or market
  • Reporting on sustainability
  • Doing something creative
  • Facing ethical scrutiny

That thread becomes your concept, and your question becomes your RQ.

Before you lock in your RQ

Pass 1: Rule out

  • Too descriptive: "What did Nike do about sweatshops?" leads to a report, not an analysis.
  • Too broad: "How has Apple been innovative?" cannot be scoped.
  • Not enough evidence: Spend 20 minutes searching. If you cannot find at least three relevant documents, reconsider the RQ.

Pass 2: Test it

  • What might an answer look like, and what information would you need?
  • Which business management tools fit?
  • How will the concept run through your analysis?
