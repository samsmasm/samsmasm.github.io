2.3

Leadership and Management

What leaders and managers do, and which style fits which situation.

Learning Goals
  • Distinguish management from leadership
  • Evaluate five leadership styles and when each works
  • Explain scientific and intuitive thinking HL

Management vs leadership

ManagementLeadership
Directing a business's resources to achieve its objectives Using strategic and creative thinking to inspire people to meet challenges and reach goals
Planning; coordinating; commanding and controlling; resolving problems Sets a vision and long-term goals; motivates; builds trust; challenges the status quo; resolves problems

Is one better? Not always. Most businesses need both, often from the same person.

Five leadership styles

StyleWhat the leader does
AutocraticMakes the decisions. Strong and rule-oriented
PaternalisticLooks after employees like family and expects loyalty
DemocraticSeeks input and involves employees in decisions
Laissez-faireHands-off: lets the team make the decisions
SituationalAdapts the style to the situation
Situational leadership

The leader adapts to the place, the time, the people and the task. No single style is always best: a crisis may call for autocratic leadership; a skilled team may work best with laissez-faire or democratic. Useful when leading a large organization with diverse needs.

Culture and ethics also shape which style works. A "know your place" society may accept autocratic leaders; a "live and let live" society may expect democratic or laissez-faire ones. Ethics can rule some choices out altogether.

Scientific and intuitive thinking HL

HL only, but useful for SL too.

ScientificIntuitive
Decisions useQuantitative research and techniquesExperience and fast reasoning, not only data
BenefitsEfficient, accurate, less biasFaster; suits emergencies
LimitsFaulty assumptions; data hard to get; slowerNot based on data; riskier
ExampleTaylor's scientific managementFord doubling wages in 1914
Recap
  • Managers direct resources to objectives; leaders inspire people towards a vision
  • Autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire, situational: each fits some situations
  • Situational leaders switch style with the place, time, people and task
  • HL: scientific thinking uses data; intuitive uses experience and speed
Activity 1: Leaders you respect
Think of a teacher, coach or club leader you really like. What do they do? Now one you find hard to work with. What do they do? What's the difference?
Activity 2: What should be done about Alex?

Alex joined your cafe team three months ago. She is polite and always on time, but orders are often wrong and tasks are left half done. Alex rarely asks for help and seems unsure what to do. Regular customers have started to complain, and other staff are covering for Alex and getting frustrated.

Write four different ideas for what should be done. Then sort them under the five leadership styles. Which style has the most? Which has none? For each style, note a strength, a risk, and a workplace where it would really work.
Activity 3: Manager or leader?
Plans next week's schedule
Develops a vision for the business in ten years
Solves a day-to-day problem
Inspires people to take on a challenge
Checks that tasks and projects are completed
Challenges the status quo
Makes sure everyone is doing their assigned duties
Builds trust in the organization
Activity 4: Review questions
Use these to guide your notes. Define each key term in your own words as you go.
  1. What is scientific thinking and management? HL
  2. What is intuitive thinking and management? HL
  3. What is the main advantage of each? HL
  4. How does management differ from leadership?
  5. What are the main functions of management?
  6. For each of autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire and situational: what is it, and when might it suit?
  7. What five factors affect situational leadership? (Hint: the mnemonic "CLOTS")
  8. How do cultural differences and ethical considerations affect leadership style?
  9. Which leadership style would you expect to use intuitive thinking? Which scientific? HL
Activity 5: Exam practice
Using a real world example, explain two differences between a leader and a manager. [4 marks]

Seven minutes. Then mark it, or swap with a partner.

Marking guide For each of the two differences: 1 mark for identifying a difference, 1 mark for applying it to an example.
Activity 6: Spot the style
Write one sentence for each style: a business or leader you know that shows it.
