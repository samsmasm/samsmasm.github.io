2.1

Introduction to Human Resource Management

What shapes HR decisions, and why people resist change.

Learning Goals
  • Explain the role of HR management
  • Explain internal and external factors that shape HR planning
  • Explain why people resist change at work
  • Evaluate HR strategies for reducing resistance to change

What HR does

RoleWhat it means
Workforce (HR) planningWorking out how many people, and which roles, the business needs
RecruitmentFinding and hiring the right people
TrainingHelping staff learn what they need to do the job well
AppraisalReviewing how staff are performing
Termination or dismissalManaging people leaving, by choice or not

Factors that shape HR planning

Internal
  • Size of the business
  • Budget for staffing
  • Promotion: who moves up, and the gaps they leave
  • Flexitime: staff choosing their hours within limits
  • Motivation and morale
External
  • Sociocultural: demographic change, changing habits, attitudes to work
  • Technological: automation can replace or redesign roles
  • Economic: unemployment, labour mobility, immigration, the gig economy
  • Legal/political: minimum wage, employment law
Pending teacher review

Demographic change, labour mobility, immigration and the gig economy were added from the syllabus to fill a gap in the lesson slides.

Why people resist change

  • Fear from misinformation or misunderstanding
  • Uncertainty over timing, impact, and who's affected
  • Self-interest: "my job matters, why change it?"
  • Low tolerance: "we're doing fine as it is"
  • Blame-shifting: "the problem is other departments"
  • Workers fear for habits and job security; managers fear backlash and conflict
The human side

Textbooks rarely sound sympathetic to employees, so here's the balance: people have put a lot of blood, sweat and tears into their workplace. Change can feel personal, like throwing out work that's already been done. And staff often feel, or know, that management is out of touch.

Reducing resistance to change

StrategyHow
ParticipationInvolve staff in discussing and deciding the change
PlanningRoll it out in stages, with feedback at each step
CommunicationKeep everyone informed, bad news as well as good
NegotiationOffer incentives that make the change easier to accept
ManipulationWin over key staff representatives so others follow
CoercionForce it through with threats; a last resort
Upside

Builds trust and cuts resistance over time. Staged planning lets managers adjust. Incentives line up staff interests with the change.

Catch

Participation and planning slow down urgent change. Resistance often stays despite communication. Manipulation and coercion damage trust and morale.

Recap
  • HR plans, recruits, trains, appraises and manages people leaving
  • HR planning is shaped by internal factors (size, budget, promotion, flexitime, motivation) and external ones (STEEPLE)
  • Resistance comes from fear, uncertainty, self-interest and low tolerance, and change can feel personal
  • Participation, planning and communication build trust but take time; coercion is fast but costly
Activity 1: Brain dump
Two minutes, no notes: list every internal and external factor affecting HR planning you can remember. Then check against the Notes tab.
Activity 2: Become the expert
Choose one factor (size, budget, promotion, flexitime, motivation, sociocultural, technological, economic, legal/political). Write:
  • A definition
  • How it affects HR planning, and which part of it
  • Why it's not a trivial issue for an HR team
  • One real-world example
  • One exam-style question about it, and a model answer
Activity 3: SwiftHaul, find the evidence

A logistics company called SwiftHaul is deciding whether to hire an additional warehouse supervisor. Internally, the company's flat structure means one existing supervisor is already stretched across three shifts, the department's budget has just been increased after a strong quarter, and the business is small enough that one bad hire would be felt company-wide. A recent internal promotion has left a gap in the team, and management knows that staff morale and motivation dip whenever shifts are understaffed. Externally, low unemployment in the region is making it harder to find qualified candidates, rising minimum wage legislation means any new hire will cost more than the last one, growing demand for next-day delivery is increasing workload pressure, and warehouse automation raises the question of whether the role should be filled by a person at all.

Find the evidence for each factor.
Check Size: small enough that one bad hire is felt company-wide.
Budget: just increased after a strong quarter.
Promotion: an internal promotion left a gap.
Motivation: morale dips when shifts are understaffed.
Economic: low unemployment, so candidates are hard to find.
Legal/political: rising minimum wage.
Sociocultural: demand for next-day delivery.
Technological: warehouse automation.
Activity 4: Uber, employee or contractor?
Use the factors to explain why Uber wants its drivers classified as contractors, and why courts in different countries haven't agreed.
Activity 5: Same change, two announcements
Version A
  • School locked down: 7am to 5pm
  • No more learning at home. All work must be done during school hours
  • All learning on paper from static textbooks. No laptops or iPads
  • Lunches charged to school accounts: compulsory. Set lunches, no options
  • Assessment every single class
  • No more real assessments (except unavoidable IB exams)
Version B
  • More learning time at school: 7am to 5pm
  • No homework. All work gets finished during school hours
  • Fewer distractions: learning on paper with carefully chosen textbooks
  • Lunches provided by the school each day. Set lunches, chef-curated daily
  • Feedback every single class
  • No more high-stakes assessments (except essential IB exams)
It's the same change. How does each version make you feel? Which reasons for resistance does Version A trigger? Which strategies does Version B use? What would it still need?
Check Version A triggers fear and uncertainty. Version B uses communication (framing, reasons). A full rollout would also explain why (evidence, what staff have seen) and ask for input on the details, such as how lunch service should run: that's participation.
Activity 6: You're the HR director
Think of a club you're actually in. As its HR director, which area (planning, recruitment, training, appraisal, dismissal) needs to change? Name the two most important internal and external factors behind that. What resistance will you meet, from which groups, and why? Describe how you'd roll out the change.
