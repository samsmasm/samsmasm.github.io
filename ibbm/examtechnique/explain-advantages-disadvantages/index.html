Exam Technique

Explain Advantages and Disadvantages

The 4-mark "explain one advantage and one disadvantage" question. Small, and everywhere — and the same skill underneath the 10-mark decision question.

Applies to
  • 5.4 Location — Velora Motors and outsourcing
  • 1.2 Types of Business Entities — private to public limited company conversion
  • A foundational skill for The 10-mark Decision Question: each option in that structure needs an advantage and a disadvantage built this way

A typical Paper 1 question

"Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of [a change] for [Company] [4 marks]" is one of the most common question shapes on Paper 1. Explain is an AO2 command term, and the question is normally split 2 marks for the advantage, 2 for the disadvantage.

How to structure it

Three steps
  • Name the theory, briefly: what is the change, in the language of the theory? This isn't itself worth marks, but it sets up everything after it.
  • Advantage: state it, explain it with reference to the case, then hammer it home with a specific detail from the case
  • Disadvantage: the same three moves
How it's marked

Typically 2 + 2: one mark for identifying a relevant advantage or disadvantage, and a second mark for explaining it in the context of this specific company. "Hammering it home" — tying the point to a specific detail or near-quote from the case — is usually what earns that second mark, not the first sentence.

Common weaknesses, from examiner reports

Subject reports on this question type keep flagging the same two failure modes. Both lose marks even when the content is factually correct.

Context without theory

Copying a fact from the stimulus without explaining the business mechanism behind it earns no knowledge marks. A report on a question about converting from a private to a public limited company noted that some students wrote only that the advantage is "they can raise finance for expansion" — lifting the phrase straight from the case. That's not explanation: knowledge is only credited once the student explains how, e.g. that a public limited company can attract new shareholders by selling shares on a stock exchange through an IPO.

Theory without context

The opposite failure: writing a generic, textbook-level point that could apply to any company, with no link back to the stimulus. The same report gave "possible loss of control" as an example — true, and worth one mark, but the second mark needs the point contextualised to this specific case (the report's example: naming the case's mention of "hostile takeovers of smaller businesses").

The pattern

Business management is an applied subject: every point needs both halves, the general theory and the case-specific practice. Reports on this question type repeatedly make the same recommendation — don't just state the advantage or disadvantage, go further and explain why it's an advantage or disadvantage using specific information, ideally a micro-quote, from the case.

Bridging the two: same point, three ways

Using Velora Motors (below) as an example — the point is "outsourcing calliper production gives Velora a cost advantage."

  • Context only (weak): "The Polish supplier can produce callipers at a lower unit cost using specialised equipment."
  • Theory only (weak): "Outsourcing can reduce costs because a specialist supplier benefits from its own economies of scale and expertise."
  • Bridged (strong): "Rising labour costs at Velora's German factory made in-house production increasingly expensive, so outsourcing to a Polish supplier with specialised equipment let Velora cut its unit cost on brake callipers below what its own factory could match, protecting its margins on every car built."
Same pattern, a different case: Kettleworks

The point is "switching to mass customisation lets Kettleworks compete on personalisation" (see 5.2 Operations Methods).

  • Context only (weak): "Competitors let customers choose colour, handle style and engraving, so Kettleworks should do the same."
  • Theory only (weak): "Mass customisation lets a business offer personalisation at close to mass-production cost, which can differentiate it from competitors."
  • Bridged (strong): "Because rivals already let customers personalise their kettles without charging much more, Kettleworks' current single white design is now a weakness rather than a cost advantage. Mass customisation would let it match that personalisation using the existing automated line with swappable options, restoring the differentiation it has lost."
Warm-up: Velora Motors

BON: Patrice Moon's leadership style
The stimulus (lines 38–50)

Patrice Moon is the site manager who supervises the construction of festival sites. She has complete control and makes all decisions without consultation. She has a span of control for four key functions. She arranged for the helicopter collection of a new water pump, and she is known to bully, shout at and threaten staff if tasks are not completed on time.

4. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage for BON of Patrice Moon's leadership style. [4]

Patrice could be described as an autocratic/authoritarian leader — but the advantage and disadvantage must be in context for both marks, not just the label.

Sample answer

Advantage: Staff are more likely to work in a way that avoids upsetting her. Keen to keep her happy, the workforce execute her direction efficiently and effectively. She gets things done, which is good for BON — "all of BON's festivals open on time." Decisions are not consulted on and can be made quickly, e.g. hiring the helicopter to collect a new water pump.

Disadvantage: Her leadership style could be deemed abusive and lead to worker dissatisfaction and demotivation. Working in a climate of fear can reduce the desire of the workforce to do a good job. Given the nature of the work, it is important to develop a good, teamwork-based approach. The negative publicity surrounding the media reports might make future recruitment tricky and could reflect poorly on BON.

Mark scheme

Marked 2 + 2. Award 1 mark for a relevant advantage/disadvantage identified, and a second mark for a relevant explanation of it in the context of BON, up to a maximum of 2 per side. Any other relevant advantage or disadvantage is accepted.

How official sample answers are written

Published sample answers like this one often blend three things: text you could actually write in an exam, a paraphrase of what a strong answer might cover, and commentary aimed at markers rather than students. Don't copy the register — copy the moves: state, explain with case detail, hammer it home.

First, try it yourself
Before reading the Velora Motors sample answer on 5.4, write your own answer to: "Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of Velora Motors' change in the production of brake callipers. [4 marks]" Use the three-step structure from the Notes tab.
Spot the weakness

Re-read the disadvantage paragraph in the BON worked example (Worked Examples tab). Mark it yourself out of 2, using the mark scheme, before revealing.

Does the disadvantage paragraph earn both marks? Why or why not?
One teacher's view This disadvantage is weak — probably 1 out of 2. "Abusive," "climate of fear," and "negative publicity" are all textbook-level or general points; only "negative publicity surrounding the media reports" gestures at the case, and it doesn't quote or name anything specific from the stimulus the way the advantage paragraph does ("all of BON's festivals open on time"). Compare the two paragraphs: the advantage hammers it home with a specific, named detail; the disadvantage doesn't quite.
