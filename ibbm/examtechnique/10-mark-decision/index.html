Exam Technique

The 10-mark Decision Question

Questions that ask you to recommend or decide between two named options, on Paper 1 Section B and Paper 2 Section B.

Applies to
  • 5.2 Operations Methods — changing production method
  • Builds on Explain Advantages and Disadvantages: each option below needs an advantage and a disadvantage built that way

Same question, two papers

Questions that ask you to recommend or decide between two named options (like "should Kettleworks change from mass/flow to mass customisation?") turn up on both Paper 1 Section B and Paper 2 Section B. They reward a specific structure.

Same, on both papers

Command terms are similar (discuss, discuss whether, explain whether, recommend whether). You need balance and application to the stimulus, and you need to name a limitation of the stimulus for top marks.

Different: how you must finish

On Paper 1 Section B, a balanced answer can score above 5/10 without picking a side. On Paper 2's 10-mark question, you must reach a justified decision between two named options: every real example of this question is framed as a choice, and the mark scheme expects you to make one.

Structure a 10-mark "should they do A or B" answer like this:

Option A / Option B structure
  • Option A: describe it, then 1–2 advantages and 1–2 disadvantages, each tied to the stimulus
  • Option B: the same treatment
  • Compare and conclusion, written last: weigh the two options against each other using this specific company's situation. Take a position
  • Limitations of the stimulus: what's missing or uncertain, and how knowing it would change or reinforce the decision

The compare/conclusion and limitations steps can swap order depending on what works for you, but the conclusion always comes last.

What a good conclusion does

It comes last and never states the answer first. It weighs the strongest points against each other: "On balance, [Company] should choose Option B because ___, although Option A offers [strength], it matters less here because ___." It ties back to this company's specific situation (cash, risk, size, timeframe). It references the limitation you named: what would confirm or change your decision. And it takes a real position, even a close one.

Before you build a whole answer: get the individual points right

Each advantage and disadvantage inside Option A and Option B needs its own state / explain / hammer-it-home structure — the most common way marks are lost here is the same as on the smaller 4-mark version of this skill. See Explain Advantages and Disadvantages for the common examiner-flagged weaknesses and worked examples.

Checklist your Kettleworks answer

Go back to the answer you wrote for 5.2, Activity 5 (Kettleworks Ltd). Tick each box your answer does, then note how you could improve it in five minutes.

Checklist: Kettleworks
  • Uses specific stimulus detail, not generic knowledge
  • One point in favour of staying with mass/flow
  • One point against staying with mass/flow
  • One point in favour of switching to mass customisation
  • One point against switching to mass customisation
  • More than one business function considered (HR, finance, marketing)
  • Specific business terminology and/or BMTs used
  • Balanced, not one-sided
  • Names a stimulus limitation and its effect on the decision
  • Ends with real synthesis, not a summary
  • Key terms used accurately
Practice, Paper 2 style — Voltway Bikes

Voltway Bikes Ltd, Paper 2 Section B Practice. Questions 1 to 6 are a finance and marketing review in short answers (10 minutes). Question 7 is the 10-mark question, using the same Option A / Option B / Compare / Limitations structure as above (20 minutes).

Before you write Q7

Plan using Option A / Option B / Limitations / Compare before you start writing. Once you've written it, go back to the checklist above and mark your own answer against it.
